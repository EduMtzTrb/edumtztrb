## 1. Purpose

This document defines the authoritative shared visual language of the Personal Platform.

It answers:

> **What visual rules make the whole platform feel like one coherent authored world?**

The platform already defines what Home, Work, Research, Lab, Art, Products, and About are, how they behave, what they contain, and how they relate. This Design System does not redesign those districts. It defines the common visual machinery through which they can express different purposes while remaining unmistakably part of one platform.

The governing distinction is:

> **The Universe documents define what each district should feel like.**  
> **The Design System defines the visual tools available to create those feelings.**

The governing district rule is:

> **One identity. Different states of resolution, density, attention, and spatial authority.**

The system therefore seeks **coherence without sameness**.

# PART I — IDENTITY SYSTEM

## 4. Identity thesis

The platform is a precise, authored architectural system whose objects may be resolved while their relationships remain open.

Its singularity does not come from isolated ingredients such as a neutral typeface, a 12-column grid, warm off-white fields, graphite backgrounds, monospace metadata, hairlines, large whitespace, or minimal shadow. Those may remain useful ingredients, but they do not constitute identity by themselves.

Identity emerges from the governed relationships between:

- resolution and openness
- order and authorship
- dominant content and latent possibility
- structural clarity and relational depth
- stable platform language and project-specific spatial authority
- concentrated void and evidence density
- common axes and selective offset
- quiet interface and deliberate activation

The platform should feel specific enough to be personally authored while remaining rigorous enough for professional decision-makers and scalable enough to accommodate future districts.

---

## 5. Identity Principle 01 — Precision without finality

### Governing idea

> **Resolved objects. Open relationships.**

### Governing sentence

> **The platform is always evolving, but never terminal.**

The platform communicates control through exact navigation, hierarchy, metadata, interaction, and state representation while preserving meaningful possibility for:

- relationship
- lineage
- branching
- reinterpretation
- transformation
- authorship
- alternative states
- evolving work

The initial experience should feel calm, resolved, trustworthy, and professionally legible.

Relational openness becomes visible only where real information exists.

### 5.1 Resolved Layer

The **Resolved Layer** is the default perceptual state of the platform.

It contains the primary content, orientation, navigation, hierarchy, visible metadata, and essential state information needed for immediate comprehension.

It should dominate first encounter.

### 5.2 Working Layer

The **Working Layer** is a secondary semantic visibility layer that may reveal real:

- relationships
- lineage
- derivation
- branch structures
- versions
- provenance
- authorship relationships or interventions
- references
- chronology
- corresponding states

The Working Layer:

- is secondary by default
- appears only where meaningful information exists
- is deliberately revealed
- can be reduced or closed
- must remain understandable without decorative technical conventions
- does not require a dedicated panel or background
- may persist where real task/context continuity requires it
- must remain accessible by keyboard and in reduced-motion conditions

The Working Layer is not permission to display fake construction geometry, fake process residue, arbitrary coordinates, or ornamental node networks.

> **Every visible trace must correspond to actual information.**

### 5.3 Controlled irregularity

Irregularity is allowed only where it strengthens meaning.

Shared consistency wins unless forcing significant content into the normal system would materially diminish its meaning.

The platform should not look unfinished merely because it values openness.

---

## 6. Identity Principle 02 — Inhabited Threshold

### Governing idea

> **From viewing to inhabiting.**

### Core rule

> **The platform owns the threshold. The record owns the interior.**

A record is first encountered as an object within the common platform. After entry, spatial authority may transfer to the record.

The general sequence is:

**Encounter → brief approach → threshold → immersion → deeper exploration → gradual return**

The threshold is perceptible but restrained. It should be felt rather than watched.

Before entry:

> the platform contains the record.

After entry:

> the record may become the environment.

A record interior may vary substantially according to its own content and spatial logic. The larger platform recedes but does not become conceptually lost.

Essential orientation remains recoverable, and leaving the record restores the **exact context the visitor left**, as governed by the approved navigation and animation systems.

The threshold must not become:

- a cinematic spectacle
- a game portal
- an obvious website effect
- a loading-state metaphor
- a mandatory WebGL/3D transition

Threshold is a transfer of **spatial authority**, not literal portal geometry.

## 7. Identity Principle 03 — Typographic Voice

> **Typography is constructed, authored, and spatial.**

Typography combines architectural discipline with human authorship. Scientific and computational qualities are secondary and emerge primarily through structure, data, relation, and information—not through software-dashboard styling.

### 7.1 Display Voice

The **Display Voice** is:

- architectural + human
- authored
- moderately distinctive
- quietly idiosyncratic rather than eccentric
- capable of behaving as architectural mass
- suitable for selective crop, offset, asymmetric placement, and unusual line breaking
- professional and legible

At large scale, display typography may shape composition as a plane or mass rather than behaving only as a conventional heading.

Controlled typographic irregularity is allowed narrowly and intentionally.

### 7.2 Reading Voice

The **Reading Voice** is:

- editorial
- measured
- highly legible
- quieter than the Display Voice
- appropriate for sustained Research and About reading
- sufficiently neutral for Work and Products

Research remains more formal and cumulative. About may become slightly more conversational while retaining professional rigor.

### 7.3 Functional Mono Voice

The **Functional Mono Voice** is used only when fixed-width notation materially improves comprehension.

Legitimate examples include:

- code
- version identifiers
- coordinates where real
- technical values
- measurements
- structured IDs
- data strings that benefit from alignment

Mono is not a brand voice and is not automatically used for dates, roles, locations, categories, labels, or metadata merely to make the platform look computational.

### 7.4 Typeface calibration boundary

Actual typeface families remain implementation-calibrated.

Typeface selection may determine **which** faces satisfy the approved roles. It may not redefine the approved typographic character.

No district may create a separate interface type system.

---

## 8. Identity Principle 04 — Signal

The **Signal** is a rare platform-wide semantic activation phenomenon.

Its perceptual character is:

- electric activation
- with a secondary spectral-interference quality
- precise
- future-scientific
- controlled
- non-theatrical

Signal is associated only with meaningful:

- connection
- branching possibility
- active or revealed authorship relationship/intervention

Ordinary ownership does **not** justify Signal. Signal must not simply mean “authored by the platform owner.”

### 8.1 Signal is not an accent color

Signal must never substitute for ordinary:

- hover
- active navigation
- selection
- focus
- CTA
- purchase action
- validation
- success
- warning
- error
- generic availability
- disabled state

Signal frequency may vary by district, but its meaning may not.

### 8.2 Signal form

Signal may be expressed through a controlled combination of:

- localized chromatic shift
- luminance
- luminous edge
- thin directional trace
- fixed spectral state
- restrained shimmer where motion is permitted

Signal should normally remain edge-scale or trace-scale. Larger manifestations are exceptional and must be justified by real content or threshold meaning.

Motion may intensify Signal. Motion may not create its meaning.

Signal must remain understandable in static and reduced-motion conditions through additional carriers such as:

- relationship geometry
- endpoint marks
- labels
- state
- structure
- textual context

Exact chromatic values remain implementation-calibrated.

---

## 9. Identity Principle 05 — Trace Language

> **Every trace encodes actual information.**

Trace language exists to expose real structure, relationship, provenance, lineage, state, and correspondence—not to simulate technical process.

### 9.1 Five semantic line families

The platform distinguishes:

1. **Structural** — boundary, frame, containment, region separation
2. **Hierarchy** — major grouping or sectional distinction
3. **Focus / State** — interaction and accessibility state
4. **Annotation** — local correspondence between note and subject
5. **Relationship / Trace** — lineage, branch, derivation, provenance, directional relationship

### 9.2 Structural versus relational linework

> **Structure is passive and boundary-oriented. Trace is relational and intentional.**

The distinction must not collapse into a simplistic dotted-versus-solid convention.

Relationship meaning may use a controlled combination of:

- endpoint marks
- origin/target differentiation
- continuity or interruption
- placement
- annotation
- Working Layer context
- Signal when justified

### 9.3 Directionality

Direction is normally conveyed through endpoint logic or differentiated origin/destination states.

Explicit arrowheads are exceptional and used only where direction would otherwise remain ambiguous.

### 9.4 Trace visibility

The Resolved Layer normally shows only minimal relational hints.

The Working Layer may progressively reveal controlled clusters of traces.

Relational complexity should unfold deliberately rather than appearing as an undifferentiated network.

Traces may cross primary content only when the relationship itself is being examined and the Working Layer is deliberately active.

No public guide, coordinate, line, construction mark, or trace may exist solely to create a technical atmosphere.

---

## 10. Identity Principle 06 — Controlled Exception

A **Controlled Exception** is permitted only when forcing significant content into the normal system would materially diminish its meaning or intrinsic spatial logic.

Curatorial importance alone is insufficient.

Authorship alone is insufficient.

### 10.1 What may bend

A justified exception may affect:

- grid behavior
- scale
- media placement
- reading direction
- local composition

Different exception types may bend different subsets of these rules.

### 10.2 Scope

An exception may occur at:

- moment level
- section level
- record level

Record-level exceptions are the rarest.

### 10.3 What may never bend

A Controlled Exception may not change or weaken:

- canonical ownership
- global route semantics
- control semantics
- Signal meaning
- trace meaning
- lifecycle/state semantics
- semantic hierarchy
- navigation clarity
- keyboard access
- focus visibility
- metadata legibility
- professional comprehension
- accessibility obligations

> **Controlled Exception can bend composition, never accessibility.**

An exception changes presentation authority, not system ownership or interaction semantics.

Repeated exceptions may not accumulate into a second visual system.

# PART II — SIGNATURE COMPOSITIONAL GRAMMAR

## 11. Governing compositional grammar

> **Controlled asymmetry, concentrated void, shared axes, selective offset, peripheral latency, and deliberate density gradients.**

Identity function:

> **The composition should feel resolved at first encounter, while retaining visible capacity for relation, depth, and further discovery.**

### 11.1 Controlled asymmetry

Compositions are intentionally imbalanced but stabilized through hierarchy, shared alignment, and proportion.

Asymmetry is not random offsetting.

One zone usually carries stronger authority while another remains relational or latent.

### 11.2 Concentrated void

Void is concentrated rather than evenly distributed.

A substantial area of emptiness may create tension against a denser occupied zone.

Void establishes:

- pause
- scale
- hierarchy
- threshold
- attention
- separation

Void is not generic luxury whitespace.

### 11.3 Shared axes

Shared architectural axes establish order and trust.

They are the primary structural reference across districts.

### 11.4 Selective offset

Selective offset introduces controlled authorship, relational tension, or secondary alignment.

It is an ordinary part of the visual grammar and is not automatically a Controlled Exception.

### 11.5 Related alignment layers

Different content layers may use related but non-identical alignment systems.

Examples include:

- primary content on the architectural grid
- metadata on a secondary axis
- annotation aligned to media
- relational traces between non-column points
- peripheral information using a related edge condition

These are not grid violations when they remain optically and semantically related to the primary structure.

### 11.6 Peripheral latency

Secondary relationships, traces, metadata, branches, or contextual information may gather at the edge of attention.

Peripheral latency is a functional role, not a fixed sidebar.

Critical orientation or transaction information may never be made latent merely for atmosphere.

### 11.7 Density gradients

Density follows evidence and informational pressure.

The system may transition between:

- concentrated void
- moderate information density
- dense evidence
- release

Complex evidence must not be artificially flattened into uniformly sparse layouts.

Sparse is not inherently more prestigious, architectural, or aligned with the identity.

### 11.8 Dominant and peripheral fields

One field normally carries primary authority while another holds latent or relational capacity.

The relationship is intentionally unequal but not inert. The peripheral zone remains active enough to create tension and possibility.

Dominance may shift during deeper exploration.

No fixed dominant/peripheral ratio is approved.

### 11.9 Edge behavior

Meaningful content may approach, align with, terminate at, or selectively exceed normal field edges when this contributes to:

- scale
- spatial tension
- record authority
- threshold
- content meaning

Edge behavior must not become a repeated stylistic trick.

# PART III — TYPOGRAPHY

## 12. Shared semantic typography hierarchy

The same semantic roles apply across all districts:

| Role | Purpose | Approved large-screen range | Character |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Display | District entrances, rare major statements | 72–120 px | Spatial, authored, sparse |
| H1 | Page, record, project, research or product identity | 44–72 px | Primary structural title |
| H2 | Major section | 32–48 px | Strong editorial division |
| H3 | Subsection or grouped module | 22–32 px | Compact structural heading |
| Body Large | Introductory or lead text | 20–24 px | Open, slower reading |
| Body | Standard prose | 16–18 px | Editorial, comfortable |
| Body Small | Supporting prose | 14–16 px | Secondary but readable |
| Caption | Media caption or credit | 13–14 px | Quiet, media-attached |
| Metadata | Structured record information | 12–13 px | Compact, precise |
| Annotation | Figure note, relationship note, technical explanation | 12–14 px | Contextual, precise |
| Label | Navigation, filter, field, control | 13–15 px | Direct, concise |

These are calibration envelopes, not commands to choose arbitrary fixed values.

Semantic hierarchy is fixed. Visual metrics may be calibrated within approved ranges.

### 12.1 Line-height ranges

- Display: 0.90–1.00
- H1: 0.98–1.08
- H2: 1.05–1.15
- H3: 1.10–1.20
- Body Large: 1.40–1.55
- Body: 1.50–1.65
- Body Small: 1.45–1.60
- Caption: 1.35–1.50
- Metadata: 1.30–1.45
- Annotation: 1.35–1.50
- Label: 1.20–1.35

Actual settings depend on the selected typefaces and require browser calibration.

### 12.2 Weight hierarchy

Default available hierarchy:

- Regular 400
- Medium 500
- Semibold 600 when necessary

Bold 700 is exceptional.

Authority should come through position, scale, spacing, and composition before excessive weight.

Maximum three simultaneous weights should normally appear in one composition.

### 12.3 Case behavior

- body and authored prose: sentence case
- authored titles: sentence/title case by context
- labels, states, and system notation: uppercase permitted selectively
- long uppercase passages: prohibited

Uppercase is functional and selective, not generic architecture-magazine styling.

### 12.4 Tracking

- Display: calibrated by family; may be slightly negative where optically appropriate
- Headings: neutral to slightly negative where appropriate
- Body: neutral
- Metadata: neutral unless notation requires otherwise
- Uppercase micro-labels: may use moderate positive spacing

Tracking values remain family-specific and implementation-calibrated.

### 12.5 Reading width

- standard prose: 55–72 characters per line
- Research long-form: preferably 60–70 characters per line
- introductory prose: may extend toward approximately 80 characters only where reading remains comfortable
- captions and metadata: naturally shorter

Full-width long-form text on wide screens is prohibited.

### 12.6 Typographic invariants

Across every district:

- semantic heading levels follow document structure
- one semantic H1 per page in normal circumstances
- normal interface text does not drop below the approved readability floor
- essential meaning may not depend only on capitalization or weight
- body text remains selectable where technically possible
- districts do not receive separate interface fonts
- responsive typography recomposes rather than mechanically scaling desktop values

# PART IV — SPACING, GRID, AND PROPORTION

## 13. Spacing infrastructure

The shared system uses a **4 px micro-unit** and an **8 px primary rhythm**.

### 13.1 Approved spacing scale

| Token | Value |
|---|---:|
| Space 0 | 0 px |
| Space 1 | 4 px |
| Space 2 | 8 px |
| Space 3 | 12 px |
| Space 4 | 16 px |
| Space 5 | 24 px |
| Space 6 | 32 px |
| Space 7 | 48 px |
| Space 8 | 64 px |
| Space 9 | 96 px |
| Space 10 | 128 px |
| Space 11 | 192 px |
| Space 12 | 256 px |

This fixed scale is infrastructure. It is not a conceptual ceiling for architectural void.

### 13.2 Token spacing

Token spacing governs reusable/local relationships such as:

- control anatomy
- labels
- metadata
- component internals
- repeated gaps
- predictable local grouping

Grouping rules:

- internal spacing < external spacing
- objects in the same semantic group sit closer than objects in different groups
- section spacing is perceptibly larger than component spacing

### 13.3 Compositional spacing

Compositional spacing governs:

- concentrated void
- thresholds
- major record anchors
- chapter transitions
- evidence gradients
- artwork isolation
- calm-to-dense transitions

Compositional spacing may be:

- fluid
- viewport-sensitive
- content-sensitive
- larger than the fixed token scale

### 13.4 Controlled-exception spacing

Spacing may depart from the ordinary composition only where content meaning justifies a Controlled Exception.

### 13.5 Governing spacing rule

> **Spacing expresses informational pressure.**

The system may move from void to dense evidence without leaving the Design System.

## 14. Shared grid system

The system uses a responsive structural column framework:

- compact: **4 columns**
- medium: **8 columns**
- wide and ultra-wide: **12 columns**

Reference thresholds and exact breakpoint boundaries remain implementation-calibrated. The 4 / 8 / 12 structural logic is fixed.

### 14.1 Margin and gutter envelopes

Existing approved ranges remain calibration envelopes:

| Context | Outer margin | Gutter |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compact | 16–24 px | 12–16 px |
| Medium | 32–48 px | 20–24 px |
| Wide | 48–80 px | 24–32 px |
| Ultra-wide | fluid | approximately 32 px or calibrated equivalent |

Exact values require browser/content testing.

### 14.2 Maximum composition width

Approximately 1600 px remains a useful standard-composition guardrail, not a universal hard boundary.

Ultra-wide screens should generally gain:

- margin
- void
- field depth

rather than stretched prose.

Full-field atmosphere and content-justified media may exceed standard composition width.

### 14.3 Primary architectural grid

The 4 / 8 / 12 system establishes:

- major anchors
- shared axes
- content organization
- cross-district structural continuity
- predictable responsive recomposition

The grid provides alignment without requiring occupancy.

### 14.4 Related alignment layers

Secondary axes for metadata, annotations, traces, peripheral information, or media relationships are legitimate when optically related to the primary grid.

### 14.5 Controlled grid exception

True grid-breaking is reserved for content-required departures such as:

- artwork-specific presentation
- intrinsic diagram structure
- meaningful project-specific spatial logic
- nonstandard reading direction
- exceptional media extent

### 14.6 Baseline rhythm

An 8 px vertical baseline should guide repeated UI and editorial structures.

Optical correction is permitted where typography or media requires it.

---

## 15. Proportional grammar

Proportion is governed by relationships, not fixed templates.

### 15.1 Content-to-void balance

Prefer occupied concentration against concentrated emptiness rather than uniform padding around every object.

### 15.2 Semantic scale authority

> **Scale follows semantic authority, not content type.**

Text, image, drawing, object, diagram, argument, or artwork may dominate depending on meaning.

No universal image/text ratio is approved.

### 15.3 Record anchor

A record interior normally begins with one dominant anchor.

The anchor may be:

- title
- image
- drawing
- artifact
- object
- proposition
- spatial field

The anchor establishes initial authority before deeper complexity unfolds.

### 15.4 Evidence zones

Dense evidence may occupy significant areas without making the entire composition uniformly dense.

Preferred progressions may include:

**calm field → evidential concentration → release**

or:

**evidence concentration → interpretive pause → further evidence**

### 15.5 Full-bleed conditions

Full bleed is permitted when:

- atmosphere needs to become environmental
- artwork requires uninterrupted authority
- spatial/immersive media genuinely needs the field
- a record interior assumes spatial authority
- semantic scale would otherwise be materially weakened

Full bleed is not a generic hero-image convention or importance marker.

# PART V — LINE, FIELD, SURFACE, GEOMETRY, AND DEPTH

## 16. Line system

### 16.1 Structural line

Purpose:

- containment
- edge
- frame
- region separation

Character:

- quiet
- continuous
- non-directional
- subordinate

### 16.2 Hierarchy line

Purpose:

- major division
- sectional distinction
- grouping hierarchy

Use sparingly. Spacing should perform most grouping work.

### 16.3 Focus / State boundary

Purpose:

- keyboard focus
- selection
- active input/control state
- validation/state feedback where appropriate

It must remain visually distinct from relational trace behavior.

### 16.4 Annotation line

Purpose:

- local label-to-target correspondence
- figure note
- specific visual reference

It must terminate clearly at its subject.

### 16.5 Relationship / Trace line

Purpose:

- canonical relationship
- lineage
- provenance
- branch
- derivation
- directional record relationship

It is normally latent or Working-Layer-associated.

### 16.6 Line rendering

Exact line weights, opacity, endpoint dimensions, and device-pixel calibration remain implementation-calibrated.

The existing v1.0 fixed line-weight table is superseded by semantic line families.

A single generic public `line-standard` role is insufficient.

### 16.7 Border rule

Prefer:

- one meaningful edge instead of four automatic edges
- shared dividers instead of repeated boxes
- tonal separation before outline
- spacing before containment

---

## 17. Fields and surfaces

> **Fields establish environment; surfaces establish local functional separation.**

### 17.1 Environmental Field

The environmental world/background condition.

Includes:

- Light Field
- Dark Field
- temporary record atmosphere

It establishes atmosphere and spatial authority.

### 17.2 Light Field

A warm-neutral light environmental family for editorial, evidential, personal, or content-led conditions.

Exact chromatic values remain calibrated.

### 17.3 Dark Field

A graphite/deep-neutral environmental family for immersive or spatially concentrated conditions.

Exact chromatic values remain calibrated.

Light and Dark Fields are authored environmental conditions, not a generic user-selectable theme system.

### 17.4 Content Surface

A local readable or functional plane used only where content needs contrast or separation from the field.

Many compositions should remain field + content without intermediate panels.

### 17.5 Functional Overlay Surface

Used for:

- modal
- navigation overlay
- temporary inspection tool
- blocking/contextual utility surface

Functional overlays must preserve clear focus and context management.

### 17.6 Focused-media Field

A temporary environmental condition that grants media greater perceptual authority while preserving recoverable controls and context.

### 17.7 Record Interior Field

A record may establish a content-led environmental condition after threshold.

This permits atmosphere variation without creating record or district branding.

### 17.8 Working Layer and surfaces

Working Layer is not a mandatory separate surface.

It may use tonal separation, traces, annotations, or local overlays where needed, but its identity is semantic rather than material.

### 17.9 Surface prohibitions

The system avoids:

- card proliferation
- glassmorphism as a primary language
- translucent panels without semantic reason
- numbered surface levels as public design meaning when semantic names are clearer

`surface-field` remains a valid semantic role.

## 18. Geometry

The platform remains predominantly rectilinear.

### 18.1 Universal geometry

Shared geometry favors:

- rectilinear construction
- stable control silhouettes
- restrained corners
- clean alignment
- restrained media boundaries
- no decorative container shapes

### 18.2 Radius

Radius is functional and restrained, never a primary identity gesture.

Exact radius values remain implementation-calibrated within this rule.

Large rounded cards and persistent pill-based UI are not part of the identity.

### 18.3 Optical correction

Optical correction is permitted where strict numeric geometry would produce visually poor alignment or balance.

Optical correction may not change semantics or accessibility.

### 18.4 Clipping and masks

Clipping or masking requires content/compositional justification.

Do not use:

- decorative blobs
- arbitrary skew
- random corner variation
- shape masks solely for visual novelty

### 18.5 Threshold geometry

Threshold should be expressed through spatial-authority transfer—field, scale, margin authority, content expansion, framing—not through literal portal geometry.

### 18.6 Project-interior departure

Project interiors may depart from ordinary geometry where content requires it, but they may not re-skin:

- navigation
- focus
- close/return
- transaction controls
- accessibility controls
- shared state semantics

---

## 19. Layering and depth

> **Layering must encode real spatial, evidential, relational, focus, or functional hierarchy.**

The approved semantic depth sequence is:

> **Environmental Field → Resolved Layer → Working Layer → Focus Layer → Functional Overlay → Critical System Layer**

### 19.1 Environmental Field

Foundational world condition.

### 19.2 Resolved Layer

Primary readable/navigable composition and initial content authority.

### 19.3 Working Layer

Relational information that may sit around, between, over, or alongside primary content without necessarily obscuring it.

### 19.4 Focus Layer

Temporary increased authority for:

- artwork
- drawing
- media
- selected evidence
- comparison
- object inspection

### 19.5 Functional Overlay

For modal and global temporary interface surfaces.

### 19.6 Critical System Layer

Implementation-level layer for critical focus visualization, system messages, or accessibility/state feedback that must remain above conflicting content.

### 19.7 Overlap

Overlap is permitted only where it represents real:

- hierarchy
- comparison
- correspondence
- foreground/background relation
- record authority
- media relation

### 19.8 Transparency

Transparency is permitted when it enables:

- retained context
- simultaneous comparison
- relational visibility
- focus recession
- genuine media properties

It must not become persistent frosted-glass styling.

### 19.9 Plane offset

Plane offset is a valid identity mechanism when it encodes:

- latent versus primary information
- evidence hierarchy
- related but distinct systems
- branching
- spatial authority

### 19.10 Shadow

Shadow remains rare and subordinate.

Depth should normally arise from:

- tonal difference
- overlap
- plane offset
- field change
- void
- scale

Exact shadow behavior, opacity values, overlap distances, and numerical z-index values remain implementation-calibrated.

# PART VI — COLOR, SIGNAL, AND STATE

## 20. Color architecture

The platform uses four semantic chromatic layers:

1. **Environmental neutrals**
2. **Content hierarchy neutrals**
3. **Functional semantic colors**
4. **Signal**

### 20.1 Environmental neutrals

Light Field and Dark Field remain warm-neutral / graphite families.

Exact values are implementation-calibrated against content, media, and accessibility.

### 20.2 Content hierarchy neutrals

Semantic roles include:

- primary text
- secondary text
- muted/tertiary text
- structural line
- hierarchy line

Muted never means illegible.

### 20.3 Functional semantic colors

Functional colors remain independent for:

- focus
- error
- warning
- success
- disabled
- interaction feedback where chromatic reinforcement is useful
- transaction-critical state where necessary

Conventional comprehension takes precedence over aesthetic purity.

Color must not be the only carrier of essential meaning.

### 20.4 Signal isolation

Signal is outside the ordinary interaction palette.

There is no approved district-accent palette.

No district-specific Signal meaning is permitted.

### 20.5 Gradients

Gradients are permitted only where they represent restrained:

- light falloff
- atmospheric depth
- field integration
- Signal spectral behavior

Promotional multicolor gradients are prohibited.

## 21. State representation

Interaction, system, lifecycle/public, and commercial state are separate dimensions.

### 21.1 Interaction state

Shared controls support as applicable:

- default
- hover
- active/pressed
- selected
- focus
- disabled
- expanded/collapsed

Selection, active route, focus, and Signal are distinct concepts.

### 21.2 System state

System states may include:

- loading
- empty
- error
- success/confirmation
- unavailable
- pending where applicable

System error must never visually redefine failed research or failed experimentation.

### 21.3 Lifecycle/public state

Lifecycle/public representation must preserve distinctions such as:

- draft
- active
- complete
- archived
- failed
- abandoned
- speculative

Public curatorial inclusion is separate from lifecycle completion.

Therefore:

- complete ≠ automatically public
- incomplete ≠ automatically private
- failed ≠ system error
- archived ≠ failed
- speculative ≠ low quality

### 21.4 Commercial state

Commercial states may include:

- forthcoming
- available
- unavailable
- limited
- discontinued

Therefore:

- unavailable ≠ archived
- discontinued ≠ archived

### 21.5 State communication priority

State should be communicated in this order:

1. clear language
2. typography
3. placement/context
4. structural treatment
5. icon where useful
6. color as reinforcement

Avoid badge proliferation.

Critical states appear at first encounter when they materially change interpretation or transaction. Secondary process states may remain metadata or deeper/Working-Layer information.

# PART VII — METADATA, ANNOTATION, AND ICONOGRAPHY

## 22. Metadata hierarchy

Metadata is one of the platform's most precise information systems.

It is organized into three tiers.

### 22.1 Primary metadata

Required for immediate orientation or professional comprehension.

Examples may include:

- title support information
- date/year where meaningful
- role
- authorship
- project/content type
- client/context
- key lifecycle state
- commercial price/availability where required

### 22.2 Secondary metadata

Useful but not required immediately.

Examples may include:

- collaborators
- disciplines
- themes
- version
- provenance
- supporting dates
- references
- additional technical values

### 22.3 Latent metadata

Appropriate for:

- deeper record disclosure
- peripheral zones
- Working Layer
- branch/version history
- extended provenance
- technical IDs
- derivation context

Latent means visually secondary, not inaccessible.

### 22.4 Metadata composition

Metadata should:

- preserve explicit label/value relationships
- use spacing before separators
- remain aligned to shared or related axes
- wrap or disclose before destructive truncation
- follow semantic order on mobile rather than preserving desktop alignment
- become dense only where evidence requires it
- avoid badge-wall or spreadsheet-dashboard appearance

### 22.5 Mono usage

Functional Mono Voice appears only where fixed-width notation genuinely helps.

Dates, roles, locations, categories, and statuses do not automatically require Mono.

### 22.6 Authorship

Authorship must be explicit where collaboration or contribution needs clarification.

It does not require repetitive self-branding on every canonically owned record.

> **Person-first authorship is expressed through judgment and continuity, not repetitive branding.**

---

## 23. Annotation language

Annotations may express:

- figure reference
- short note
- local leader line
- real measurement
- real axis or coordinate
- sequence marker
- state label
- source/reference marker

Annotations must remain understandable without architectural training.

They may never become ornamental pseudo-technical graphics.

---

## 24. Iconography

Icons are:

- geometric
- restrained
- optically balanced
- consistent in visual family
- subordinate to text where ambiguity is plausible

Existing icon size envelopes may be retained as implementation references:

- 16 × 16 px compact
- 20 × 20 px standard-small
- 24 × 24 px standard

Exact stroke calibration remains implementation-level.

Text labels are required whenever an icon is not universally obvious.

Emoji are not interface icons.

# PART VIII — CONTROLS AND SHARED INTERACTION LANGUAGE

## 25. Governing control rule

> **One stable control language persists across the entire platform and all record interiors.**

Project interiors may alter:

- prominence
- placement
- degree of recession

They may not redefine:

- meaning
- focus behavior
- selected-state behavior
- keyboard behavior
- icon semantics
- disabled behavior
- feedback semantics
- geometry conventions
- accessible naming

> **Controls may leave immediate attention; they may not leave reliable reach.**

---

## 26. Navigation family

Shared navigation includes:

- global universe navigation
- district navigation/marker where appropriate
- local navigation
- breadcrumb/route indication where appropriate
- previous/next
- return
- Search entry
- Index entry
- mobile navigation

Every page must allow a visitor to determine:

- where they are
- what district owns the current record/context
- how to return to the common platform
- how to continue locally

---

## 27. Links

### 27.1 Inline link

Used inside prose and distinguishable without color alone.

### 27.2 Route link

Used to move between spaces, records, chapters, or districts.

May use a directional cue, line, index, or other shared semantics.

### 27.3 Utility link

Used for secondary functions such as download, copy, source, share, legal information, or support.

Link meaning must remain clear without animation or Signal.

---

## 28. Buttons

The platform retains three action levels:

### Primary action

For the most important local action.

- strong hierarchy
- rectilinear geometry
- concise label
- generally one primary action per local group

### Secondary action

Same basic control language with quieter hierarchy.

### Tertiary / text action

Lower-priority exploratory action without a containing surface by default.

### Button invariants

Buttons define, as applicable:

- default
- hover
- focus-visible
- pressed
- disabled
- loading

Signal is not a primary-action treatment.

---

## 29. Icon controls

Icon-only controls are permitted only when the function is immediately recognizable or has an accessible label.

Interactive targets should normally provide at least a 44 × 44 px hit area even when the visible icon is smaller.

Examples include:

- close
- previous
- next
- expand
- play/pause
- zoom
- menu

Critical or destructive actions require visible text when ambiguity is plausible.

---

## 30. Search and Index

Search and Index are distinct global utilities.

They may share typography, geometry, focus, field, and control primitives, but they must not appear as interchangeable states of one function.

### Search communicates

- query
- retrieval
- matching
- directed lookup

### Index communicates

- structured browsing
- corpus orientation
- systematic overview

Their labels, interaction structures, and state models must remain distinct.

---

## 31. Filters and lenses

Filters and lenses may share control primitives but must preserve different semantics.

A selected filter must not appear to be:

- canonical navigation
- district ownership
- a lens

A selected lens must not appear to be:

- route ownership
- a filter

Visual consistency does not permit semantic collapse.

---

## 32. Working Layer control

The Working Layer control is a stable platform control pattern with contextual availability.

Rules:

- it appears only where meaningful Working Layer information exists
- its state is perceivable without Signal
- its accessible label communicates action/state clearly
- its state may persist where approved navigation/spatial-memory behavior requires
- record interiors may not invent a different toggle language

Working Layer is a platform capability, not a mandatory feature of every record.

---

## 33. Forms

Forms remain deliberately conventional.

Every field provides:

- persistent visible label
- control
- optional guidance
- focus state
- error state
- error explanation where needed

Placeholder text cannot replace a field label.

Completion clarity outranks atmosphere.

---

## 34. Product transaction controls

Products may increase the prominence of:

- purchase
- inquiry
- configuration
- quantity where applicable
- availability-related actions

These remain within the shared:

- control family
- typography
- geometry
- focus system
- state model
- feedback semantics

No separate ecommerce interaction language is permitted.

Signal is not a purchase CTA color.

---

## 34.1 Shared record / plate family

The platform does not use one generic card as the universal container for content. Shared record previews are treated as records, plates, portals, or objects according to their task.

Approved shared families include:

### Portal record

Purpose: entry into a district or major collection.

Typical emphasis:

- strong spatial field
- concentrated void
- minimal essential information
- clear route cue

### Media record

Purpose: preview visual Work, Art, Lab, Product, or other media-led records.

Typical emphasis:

- media authority according to content
- title
- Primary metadata only
- optional concise supporting text

### Index record

Purpose: scanning Search results, Index results, archives, or structured record lists.

Typical emphasis:

- alignment
- compact hierarchy
- minimal containment
- high scan efficiency

Index records must not visually imply duplicate canonical ownership.

### Editorial record

Purpose: Research, paper, essay, question, note, or publication-led preview.

Typical emphasis:

- text-led hierarchy
- state/date/source where meaningful
- controlled measure
- restrained relational hints

### Product record

Purpose: commercial object or offer preview.

Typical emphasis:

- object identity
- price/availability where relevant
- edition/variant where relevant
- clear transaction route

All record families share the same platform typography, state semantics, control behavior, focus system, accessibility, and responsive logic. They are not separate component brands.

Essential information may not exist only on hover. Records should not make dramatic decorative scale jumps on hover/focus.

## 34.2 Metadata rail

A metadata rail is an optional shared layout device for structured secondary information.

It may appear beside, above, below, or within a record according to composition and viewport. It is a role rather than a fixed sidebar.

It uses the Primary / Secondary / Latent metadata hierarchy and may become sequential or disclosed on compact screens.

It must not become a dashboard column or force all records into the same layout.

## 35. Overlays

Functional overlays include:

- modal
- lightbox
- drawer
- full-screen media viewer
- mobile navigation layer

They must provide:

- visible close/recovery
- keyboard dismissal where appropriate
- focus management
- correct focus return
- background interaction prevention where modal
- clear relation to underlying context

# PART IX — MEDIA BEHAVIOR

## 36. Governing media rules

> **Scale follows semantic authority, not media type.**

> **The platform may frame evidence; it may not falsify it.**

The media system supports:

- still images
- photographs
- architectural drawings
- diagrams
- renders
- documents/PDFs
- code/output
- data visualizations
- video
- audio where applicable
- 3D/interactive media where applicable
- product imagery
- artwork
- comparisons
- mixed-media sequences

---

## 37. Media authority

Media gains authority through:

- semantic importance
- scale
- placement
- surrounding void
- field relationship
- focus/layering
- threshold context

Visual media does not automatically outrank text, argument, diagram, or object information.

---

## 38. Aspect ratio and fidelity

Native media extent should generally be respected where boundary or geometry carries meaning.

### 38.1 High-fidelity / crop-resistant media

Normally preserve native extent or provide an uncropped inspection state:

- architectural drawings
- plans/sections/elevations
- technical evidence
- meaning-bearing diagrams
- artworks
- documents
- evidence-sensitive product detail
- data visualizations

### 38.2 Moderately crop-tolerant media

May be composed more freely where meaning survives:

- contextual photography
- process imagery
- atmospheric imagery
- selected editorial photography
- secondary archival material

### 38.3 Governing crop rule

> **Cropping is a compositional operation, not a corrective default.**

Cropping must never:

- remove critical evidence
- misrepresent artwork
- change drawing/document meaning
- hide required product information
- distort authorship

Where fidelity matters, a complete representation should remain readily available.

---

## 39. Drawings and technical evidence

Preserve where relevant:

- original line hierarchy
- drawing annotations
- scale relationships
- legends
- keys
- information-bearing color
- provenance/source

The interface may control:

- surrounding field
- display scale
- caption placement
- focus/zoom access
- comparison context

It may not cosmetically rewrite evidence merely to resemble the interface.

---

## 40. Artwork

Artwork receives the strongest protection from interface competition.

Default rules:

- preserve artwork boundaries/aspect ratio unless the work itself requires another behavior
- avoid Signal competing chromatically with the work
- metadata may recede during primary encounter
- focused-media field may become extremely quiet
- sequence may follow artwork logic
- essential controls remain recoverable

Controlled Exceptions in Art exist for the artwork, not for platform decoration.

---

## 41. Product imagery

Product media must support evaluation through some combination of:

- primary object view
- detail views
- material views
- scale/context where useful
- configuration/variant views
- technical imagery

The platform is not required to imitate a conventional ecommerce thumbnail gallery if another shared media pattern serves evaluation clearly.

---

## 42. Captions and provenance

Captions remain visually quiet and spatially associated with their media.

They may include:

- figure/object identifier
- concise description/title
- date
- medium
- source
- provenance
- authorship
- technical note
- relationship/reference

Credits may not exist only on hover.

Functional Mono appears only where notation benefits from it.

---

## 43. Video and moving media

Default video playback is user-initiated.

Autoplay is permitted only when:

- motion is non-essential
- audio is absent/muted
- it does not interfere with reading/navigation
- reduced-motion preferences are respected
- pause is available where required
- it is justified by content rather than generic immersion

Video requires a meaningful static/poster representation when not active.

Meaningful speech/audio requires captions/transcripts or equivalent access as appropriate.

Background movement is extremely rare.

---

## 44. 3D and interactive media

The architectural-metaverse concept does not require WebGL/3D.

Where 3D/interactive media is used:

- basic record comprehension may not depend solely on it
- non-interactive alternatives preserve essential meaning where feasible
- platform controls remain distinct from content interaction
- keyboard/touch alternatives are required where interaction is essential
- performance failure may not destroy record access or navigation
- loading state remains explicit
- the visitor may not be trapped inside the interaction

---

## 45. Comparison media

Comparison is a shared pattern supporting:

- before/after
- versions
- alternatives
- evidence states
- Lab tests
- Work documentation
- derivation

The relationship must remain understandable through:

- labels
- stable item identity
- spatial correspondence
- sequencing
- trace/relationship semantics where relevant

Animation or dragging may enhance comparison but may not be the only means of understanding it.

# PART X — RESPONSIVE LOGIC

## 46. Governing responsive principle

> **Responsive design preserves semantic relationships, hierarchy, latency, and spatial authority rather than desktop coordinates.**

The platform does not create a separate mobile visual identity.

---

## 47. Structural recomposition

The 4 / 8 / 12 grid remains shared across responsive conditions.

On narrow screens:

- desktop coordinates may change
- shared-axis logic remains
- selective offsets recompose
- lateral relationships may become vertical
- concentrated void may compress
- evidence density reorganizes
- peripheral latency may become sequential latency

---

## 48. Responsive asymmetry

Asymmetry is a hierarchy relationship, not a desktop coordinate pattern.

On narrow screens it may survive through:

- unequal vertical intervals
- different content widths
- scale contrast
- selective edge occupation
- offset metadata
- delayed secondary information
- uneven density

Arbitrary indentation is not a valid substitute for desktop asymmetry.

---

## 49. Concentrated void on small screens

Void may compress where it begins to:

- obstruct navigation
- push critical information unnecessarily away
- make Work/Products inefficient
- separate evidence from explanation
- consume disproportionate shallow viewport depth

Home and Art may retain stronger calm than Work or Products, but identity is preserved through attention and hierarchy rather than literal pixel volume.

---

## 50. Sequential latency

On wide screens, latency may be spatial.

On narrow screens, peripheral information may become:

- later in sequence
- expandable
- contextual
- secondary disclosure
- Working Layer content

This is an approved transformation of hierarchy, not loss of identity.

---

## 51. Layering collapse

Where overlap becomes harmful:

- overlapping planes may become sequential
- relational overlays may become inline/expandable
- peripheral metadata may become disclosure
- comparisons may become stepped/swipe/stack structures
- traces may reroute vertically
- focused media may become viewport-width

The semantic depth hierarchy remains intact even when literal depth flattens.

Do not convert every plane into a generic card.

---

## 52. Mobile threshold

Mobile still communicates:

**Encounter → brief approach → transfer of spatial authority → immersion → deeper exploration → exact return**

It may rely more on:

- scale
- field
- hierarchy
- content authority
- interface recession

and less on literal lateral displacement.

No gesture may be the sole path to enter, leave, return, close, or reveal essential Working Layer information.

---

## 53. Responsive typography

Responsive typography preserves:

- semantic rank
- Display Voice character
- Reading Voice legibility
- readable measure
- metadata hierarchy

Do not mechanically scale every desktop role by a fixed percentage.

Line breaks, crop, width, and scale contrast may recompose.

---

## 54. Dense evidence on mobile

Work, Research, and Lab prioritize:

1. readable text
2. evidence identity
3. relationship labels
4. comparison logic
5. original spatial arrangement where possible

Controlled horizontal pan/scroll is permitted only for content that intrinsically requires width, such as large drawings, data tables, or technical diagrams.

Do not solve density by:

- shrinking everything
- generic card stacking
- hiding essential evidence
- flattening meaningful distinctions

---

## 55. Touch and control recovery

Touch target size and control recovery take priority over visual recession.

Essential controls may require greater visible presence on mobile than desktop.

Products must keep transaction-critical information readily reachable.

Art may retain strong interface recession while maintaining accessible recovery.

---

# PART XI — ACCESSIBILITY

## 56. Accessibility baseline

The platform targets **WCAG 2.2 AA** as the minimum shared standard.

This applies across:

- all districts
- all record interiors
- Controlled Exceptions
- Working Layer
- Signal
- responsive transformations
- Focus Layer
- overlays

> **No Controlled Exception may waive accessibility requirements.**

---

## 57. Typography and zoom

The system must preserve:

- readable default sizes
- sufficient line height
- readable text measure
- hierarchy under zoom
- reflow
- no essential clipping/loss
- long-form readability

The platform remains usable at 200% zoom and must support required reflow behavior at greater enlargement where applicable.

Controlled display irregularity may not make essential words unreadable.

---

## 58. Color and contrast

Interface text, controls, focus, and essential graphics must meet applicable WCAG 2.2 AA contrast requirements.

Signal never carries meaning through color alone.

Functional state colors require redundant semantic carriers where meaning is important.

Record-specific atmosphere remains inside an accessibility envelope.

Artwork itself is not an interface control, but controls placed over/around it must remain accessible.

---

## 59. Focus

Focus is mandatory and independent of Signal.

It must:

- remain immediately visible
- work on Light and Dark Fields
- work over record-specific atmosphere
- remain above overlapping content where necessary
- be distinguishable from selected state and Signal
- not be clipped by containers
- survive responsive reflow

Exact focus rendering remains implementation-calibrated subject to these constraints.

---

## 60. Keyboard

All platform controls and essential content interactions are keyboard operable.

Requirements include:

- logical order
- no keyboard traps
- Working Layer accessibility
- Focus Layer usability
- overlay dismissal
- recoverable return
- platform controls available inside project interiors

No district may create an essential gesture-only path.

---

## 61. Semantic reading order

> **Visual offset may change perception; it may not corrupt semantic sequence.**

Semantic/document order follows meaning rather than literal visual position.

This is especially important for:

- selective offsets
- peripheral latency
- Research annotations
- layered evidence
- Lab branches
- responsive recomposition

Metadata and annotations moved visually remain programmatically associated with their subjects.

---

## 62. Motion accessibility

The static system remains understandable without animation.

Reduced-motion conditions preserve:

- state
- hierarchy
- threshold meaning
- Signal meaning
- relationship meaning
- return context

No shimmer, transition, or spatial travel is required to understand structure.

---

## 63. Media alternatives

Design-System obligations include:

- informative images receive appropriate alternative text
- decorative geometry is hidden from assistive reading
- complex diagrams receive concise alternatives and extended explanation where necessary
- video speech receives captions
- audio-led content receives transcript/equivalent access
- meaningful visual-only video information receives appropriate alternative description where necessary
- interactive media provides an alternative path to essential information

The Design System defines the obligation for an equivalent, not the exact editorial wording.

---

## 64. Touch

Interactive targets should normally provide at least a 44 × 44 px hit area.

Requirements include:

- adequate target separation
- no hover-only functions
- alternatives to precision gestures
- visible/recoverable controls during immersive states

---

## 65. Programmatic state

Selected, expanded, disabled, error, and other interactive states must be programmatically exposed where applicable.

Lifecycle/public/commercial state must remain textually understandable.

---

## 66. Overlays and focus layers

Overlays and Focus Layer states preserve:

- focus management
- dismissal
- background interaction rules
- reading order
- focus return
- exact-context recovery

---

## 67. Working Layer accessibility

The Working Layer is an informational system, not a visual effect.

It must:

- be keyboard accessible
- expose relationship types textually or structurally
- not require following a line visually
- preserve origin/target identity
- permit closure/reduction
- work without Signal
- work without motion
- recompose on narrow screens
- not irrecoverably obscure primary content

---

## 68. Cognitive accessibility

Exploratory depth remains optional.

The system prohibits:

- hidden essential information
- ambiguous icon-only critical actions
- unexpected navigation relocation
- excessive simultaneous signals
- essential information available only through spatial experimentation

A direct path must remain available whenever an exploratory path exists.

---

# PART XII — DISTRICT EXPRESSION

## 69. Shared district rule

> **One identity. Different states of resolution, density, attention, and spatial authority.**

Districts vary by degree rather than vocabulary.

They may vary:

- concentrated void
- information density
- Working Layer availability
- trace visibility
- peripheral latency
- selective offset frequency
- layering/depth
- interface recession
- metadata prominence
- evidence density
- immersion potential
- Controlled Exception tolerance
- reading rhythm
- balance between dominant/peripheral zones

They may not independently redefine:

- typography
- Signal semantics
- trace semantics
- controls
- accessibility
- state semantics
- threshold logic
- authorship logic
- canonical ownership

No district branding palette is approved.

---

## 70. Home — synthesis + latency

Home is the strongest synthesis of the common platform identity.

Primary tendencies:

- resolved + relational
- strongest concentrated void
- low default evidence density
- high peripheral latency
- low Working Layer visibility
- rare Signal
- low–moderate layering
- clear professional orientation

Home must communicate one multidisciplinary authored practice before categories.

## 71. Work — resolution + evidence

Primary tendencies:

- strongest or near-strongest Resolved Layer
- strong shared axes
- calm overview → dense evidence
- high role/authorship/evidence clarity
- restrained Working Layer
- low–moderate layering
- very rare–rare Signal
- moderate Controlled Exception tolerance

- interpretive + relational

- moderate → dense information
- high reference/annotation presence
- higher Working Layer availability than Work

- rare Signal

## 73. Lab — provisionality + Working Layer activity

- more selective offset
- moderate–strong layering
- highly variable density

- highest Controlled Exception tolerance
- strong immersion potential

Lab exposes more of the same system, not a different system.

It must remain legible, testable, and controlled rather than visually chaotic.

## 74. Art — recession + attention

Primary tendencies:

- atmospheric + resolved encounter
- strongest interface recession
- concentrated void
- very low visible metadata at primary encounter
- very low Working Layer visibility
- very rare Signal
- minimal interface layering
- strong immersion
- high exception tolerance when required by the artwork

The system expresses itself through recession rather than competition.

---

## 75. Products — evaluation + transaction

Primary tendencies:

- resolved + transactional
- moderate efficient density
- strong Primary metadata
- low Working Layer visibility
- strong shared axes
- low–moderate layering
- very rare Signal
- low–moderate exception tolerance

Commercial clarity may increase without creating a separate ecommerce brand.

Transaction-critical information may not become latent.

---

## 76. About — authorship + continuity

Primary tendencies:

- biographical + interpretive
- editorial/human reading rhythm
- moderate density
- moderate relational depth
- low–moderate layering
- rare Signal
- more human selective offset than Work/Products where appropriate

About remains person-first and may not simulate a corporate studio identity.

# PART XVII — TOKEN MODEL

## 90. Token philosophy

A closed Design System does not require every implementation token to have an exact value.

Tokens encode reusable semantic decisions, not every compositional relationship.

Approved classifications are:

- **Resolved exact value**
- **Resolved semantic role**
- **Resolved range**
- **Fluid / formula-based**
- **Implementation-calibrated**
- **Not appropriate as a token**
- **Remove / replace**

A token may be approved by semantic role while its numeric/color value remains intentionally unresolved.

---

## 91. Typography token model

Semantic roles:

- `font-display`
- `font-reading`
- `font-mono-functional`
- `type-display`
- `type-h1`
- `type-h2`
- `type-h3`
- `type-body-large`
- `type-body`
- `type-body-small`
- `type-caption`
- `type-metadata`
- `type-annotation`

# PART XIX — QUALITY CONTROL

## 106. Coherence test

A design belongs to the platform only if it:

- uses the approved typographic roles
- follows shared grid logic
- uses the shared spacing infrastructure
- expresses asymmetry/offset within the approved grammar
- uses semantic line families correctly
- uses shared color/state semantics
- preserves Signal meaning
- uses shared control behavior
- respects accessibility
- differentiates districts through degree rather than replacement

---

## 107. Hierarchy test

At any major viewport, a visitor should be able to identify:

1. where they are
2. what is primary
3. what supports it
4. what can be acted upon
5. what state materially affects interpretation
6. where they can go next

---

## 108. Restraint test

For every visual element ask:

- Does it clarify?
- Does it orient?
- Does it establish hierarchy?
- Does it support atmosphere?
- Does it communicate real state or relationship?
- Does it support content?

If none apply, remove it.

---

## 109. Trace authenticity test

Any technical-looking mark must answer:

- What real information does this encode?
- What relationship/state/provenance does it represent?
- Would removing it remove meaning?

If there is no factual answer, it does not belong.

---

A district is successfully differentiated when it remains distinct even if:

- Signal never appears
- color is reduced to neutral
- typography remains shared

Its character should emerge from:

- resolution
- void
- density
- latency
- media authority
- metadata prominence
- trace visibility
- layering
- immersion
- spatial authority

- generic architecture portfolio
- generic Swiss/minimal portfolio

- no fake technical graphics

# PART XXI — SUPERSESSION NOTE

## 117. What changed from v1.0

`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md v1.1` retains the functional foundation of v1.0 but replaces generic identity assumptions with a more singular authored system.

Major changes include:

1. **Universal Inter is retired as the approved authored type model.**  
   v1.1 defines Display Voice, Reading Voice, and Functional Mono roles; actual families remain calibrated.

2. **IBM Plex Mono is no longer the default metadata aesthetic.**  
   Mono is restricted to information that materially benefits from fixed-width notation.

3. **Signal is redefined.**  
   It is no longer a general accent/state/district marker. It is a rare semantic activation phenomenon tied to meaningful connection, branching, or active/revealed authorship relation/intervention.

4. **District accent palettes are removed.**  
   Districts differentiate through resolution, density, latency, layering, evidence, atmosphere, and spatial authority—not branding colors.

5. **The signature compositional grammar is formalized.**  
   Controlled asymmetry, concentrated void, shared axes, selective offset, peripheral latency, and deliberate density gradients now define recognizable composition.

6. **Resolved Layer / Working Layer become formal identity structures.**  
   The Working Layer is semantic and factual, not decorative technical residue.

7. **Linework is semantically separated.**  
   Structural, hierarchy, focus/state, annotation, and relationship/trace roles replace one generic architectural hairline model.

8. **Fields and surfaces are clarified.**  
   Fields establish environment; surfaces provide local functional separation. Numbered surfaces no longer define public visual meaning.

9. **Controlled Exception receives explicit governance.**  
   Exceptions are content-based and cannot change accessibility, ownership, state, route, Signal, trace, or control semantics.

10. **Responsive identity is strengthened.**  
    Mobile preserves relationships, hierarchy, latency, and authority rather than literal desktop geometry or generic stacked cards.

11. **State representation becomes explicitly multidimensional.**  
    Lifecycle, public, commercial, interaction, and system states do not collapse into one status/badge system.

12. **Token governance is made explicit.**  
    Design-System closure is semantic; implementation-calibrated values remain bounded and may not become permission to redesign.
