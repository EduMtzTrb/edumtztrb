# Personal Platform — Cross-Universe System Requirements

**Document:** `CROSS_UNIVERSE_SYSTEM_REQUIREMENTS.md`
**Version:** 1.0
**Scope:** The shared obligations extracted from the seven approved Universe v1.1 specifications — the requirements every district must satisfy, and the requirements the future Design System, Animation System, and Platform Specification must in turn satisfy.

> Work demonstrates capability.
> Research develops understanding.
> Lab tests and transforms.
> Art concentrates perception.
> Products operationalizes value.
> About reveals authorship, continuity, and intent.
> Home makes the universe believable.

> **Universe documents define district character. Cross-universe requirements define shared obligations.**
> **Coherence does not require sameness.**

---

## 1. Purpose

This document exists because seven independently governed district specifications share a foundation that none of them individually owns. Home's silence baseline, Work's evidence hierarchy, Research's dual navigation, Lab's honesty about failure, Art's spatial restraint, Products' commercial clarity, and About's professional-first audience are not seven unrelated design choices — they are seven applications of the same small set of underlying laws. This document names those laws once, so the Design System, Animation System, and Platform Specification inherit them consistently instead of each rediscovering — or worse, each redefining — them independently.

**What it governs:** the requirements shared across two or more districts, and the obligations the future system-level documents must satisfy in translating district specifications into a working platform.

**What it does not govern:**

- the identity, atmosphere, or internal boundaries of any single district — those remain owned by that district's own `*_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` document;
- exact visual tokens, typography, color values, spacing values, grid measurements, or breakpoints;
- exact motion durations or easing curves;
- technical schemas, frameworks, storage, analytics, or payment infrastructure;
- individual content — no project, artwork, research line, experiment, product, or biography is defined or implied here.

**Relationship to governing documents:**

- `Project Instructions.txt` sets the project's phase discipline and decision-status vocabulary; this document inherits both without modification.
- `PLATFORM_VISION.md` sets identity, positioning, and audience priority; this document extracts the *shared behavioral consequences* of that identity, not a new identity.
- `INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md` sets routes, hierarchy, canonical-record logic, and navigation structure; this document restates IA's cross-district rules as binding requirements alongside the districts' own reinforcement of them, and resolves the one point IA left open (legal/administrative ownership) using the resolution already recorded in About v1.1 and Products v1.1.
- Each Universe v1.1 document remains the authority on its own district's character. Where a requirement here and a district document differ in emphasis, the district document governs its own territory; this document governs what districts share.
- This document is upstream of and constrains `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`, `ANIMATION_SYSTEM.md`, and `PLATFORM_SPECIFICATION.md` — none of which exists yet. It states what those documents must satisfy; it does not write them.

**This document does not:**

- redesign any district;
- define exact tokens;
- define implementation;
- replace any Universe document.

## 3. Shared Platform Identity

> Work demonstrates capability.
> Research develops understanding.
> Lab tests and transforms.
> Art concentrates perception.
> Products operationalizes value.
> About reveals authorship, continuity, and intent.
> Home makes the universe believable.

These are not marketing taglines. They are placement and ownership tests — the sentence a reviewer should be able to complete when deciding which district a piece of content belongs in.

The shared identity beneath all seven districts:

- **One coherent practice.** The platform represents a single multidisciplinary design intelligence, not seven unrelated interests wearing a shared header.
- **One architectural universe.** Home establishes the world; every district is a territory within it, not a separate site.
- **Distinct districts with shared foundations.** Districts differ in character, pacing, and emphasis — never in accessibility, orientation logic, canonical-ownership rules, or professional clarity.
- **Professional decision-makers as the primary audience.** Per `PLATFORM_VISION.md` and formalized in About v1.1 §5, employers, clients, collaborators, and institutions are the primary audience across the platform, not only within About. Where professional clarity and atmospheric or exploratory ambiguity would compete, professional clarity governs.
- **Deeper exploratory visitors, supported through progressive disclosure.** A secondary audience is rewarded for lingering, never required to.
- **Architectural-metaverse language is organizational and experiential, not literal.** Every district's own document explicitly rules out literal city, building, game, or virtual-tour simulation. This document treats that as a shared, non-negotiable law, not a district-by-district preference.
- **Long-term evolution toward personal practice, studio, or company.** Work, Products, and About each define their own path from individual authorship toward collective or organizational representation; the shared requirement is that this evolution must be additive — extending the system, never requiring its foundational redesign.
- **What the platform must never become:** a generic portfolio, a design-agency site, a marketplace, an academic profile, a gallery template, or a technology demonstration. Each district's own document names its version of this list; the shared version is the union of all seven, because any one district drifting into one of these generic identities weakens the coherence of the whole.

## 5. Shared Spatial Requirements

### 5.1 Shared vocabulary

threshold · field · frame · line · plane · axis · datum · trace · void · depth · scale · horizon · light · shadow · alignment · rhythm · sequence · pause · focus · passage · return · spatial memory

These terms describe spatial *roles*, not decorative motifs. A district may never use one of these words to mean something other than its role here.

### 5.2 Shared obligations

## CUR-SPA-01 — One world, several districts

The platform must feel like one architectural universe rather than several separately branded websites. District identity must emerge through controlled variation in density, scale, pacing, light, proximity, framing, information emphasis, media treatment, and interaction rhythm. Districts must not create unrelated visual brands, independent token systems, or incompatible navigation models.

## CUR-SPA-02 — Abstract architectural language

Architecture must operate as a system of proportion, sequence, hierarchy, adjacency, threshold, and orientation. It must not become a literal city, a simulated building, a virtual tour, a game world, a decorative three-dimensional environment, or a technology spectacle.

## CUR-SPA-03 — Shared spatial vocabulary

The vocabulary in §5.1 describes spatial roles, not decorative motifs.

## CUR-SPA-04 — Silence is the baseline

The default environmental condition is calm and restrained. Silence means controlled reduction of competing signals, not absence of information. It must never obscure current location, available actions, content status, media state, exit or return controls, or direct access.

## CUR-SPA-05 — Atmosphere supports meaning

Atmosphere may deepen attention, orientation, evidence, inquiry, experimentation, perception, exchange, or authorship according to district purpose. Atmosphere must never delay essential access, hide professional or commercial information, require animation, depend on poor performance, substitute ambiguity for structure, or make preliminary material appear more resolved than it is.

## CUR-SPA-06 — Curated foreground and comprehensive depth

Every content-bearing district must provide a curated, edited foreground that communicates identity and current significance, and a comprehensive archive, catalogue, Index, or equivalent precise view. The homepage or district entrance must not become the complete archive. The archive must not replace curation.

## CUR-SPA-07 — Variable composition over uniform grids

The shared system must support variable scale, rhythm, proportion, and grouping. A universal card grid must not become the governing presentation model. Uniform grids may be used where systematic scanning is the task, such as an Index, catalogue, comparison view, or archive.

## CUR-SPA-08 — Void is functional

Empty space must communicate hierarchy, pause, concentration, separation, capacity for growth, or a change of state. It must not read as missing content, broken layout, or an implementation placeholder.

## CUR-SPA-09 — Content determines local conditions

Media and content may require different proportions, densities, or viewing conditions. The shared system must accommodate those differences without forcing one crop, one aspect ratio, one card anatomy, one media frame, or one reading depth.

## CUR-SPA-10 — Architecture recedes at focus

When a visitor enters a focused state, the surrounding system must reduce competing signals while preserving orientation and exit. This is especially important for artwork encounters, project evidence, research artifacts, experiment comparisons, product evaluation, and biographical reading.

### 5.3 District transformation

The shared vocabulary and obligations above are interpreted, not repeated, by each district: Home's field is open and centerless; Work's field is denser and more aligned; Research's field is diffuse and annotated; Lab's field is instrumented and provisional; Art's field is reduced to near-silence; Products' field is a showroom with a counter; About's field is intimate and proximate. Same laws, different degree — never a different law.

### 5.4 Prohibited standardization

The shared system must not impose one layout, one density, one media treatment, one card anatomy, one spatial composition, or one atmosphere on every district. Standardizing the *laws* is the goal; standardizing the *expression* of those laws is the failure mode this document exists to prevent.

## 6. Navigation Requirements

Every meaningful view must communicate: current location, current district, current hierarchy, active lens or mode, available destinations, the consequence of an action, whether an action changes level, context, or district, and how to return.

| Requirement | Applies to |
|---|---|
| Global navigation reaches Home and every district from anywhere | All seven districts |
| District navigation follows the common conceptual order: overview/threshold → curated/current content → organizational groupings or lenses → comprehensive archive/catalogue/Index → Search | All seven districts (labels may vary) |
| Contextual navigation names parent section, collection, series, territory, family, or lineage | Work, Research, Lab, Art, Products |
| Spatial navigation and direct navigation are equally legitimate, never sequential | Home, Research, Lab, Art |
| Index and Search remain global utilities, not districts | Platform-wide |
| Breadcrumbs or an equivalent semantic mechanism exist for deep structures | All seven districts |
| Position is preserved on return from focus, filter, or detail | All seven districts |
| Previous/next controls require a real, intentional sequence | Work, Research, Lab, Art |
| No essential route depends on hover, animation, precise pointing, spatial interpretation, sound, gesture discovery, or a large viewport | Platform-wide |
| Mobile navigation remains direct and complete | Platform-wide |
| Full keyboard navigation | Platform-wide |

## CUR-NAV-01 — Exploration and direct access are equal

Spatial exploration and direct access are both first-class navigation modes. Exploration may enrich access. It may never be required for access.

## CUR-NAV-02 — Stable primary navigation

The permanent primary district labels are Home, Work, Research, Lab, Art, Products, About. These labels must remain conceptually stable across the platform.

## CUR-NAV-03 — Global utilities remain global

Search, Index, Contact, language selection (when activated), and legal and administrative information are utilities, not districts. They must remain reachable without requiring a district-specific journey.

## CUR-NAV-04 — Shared district-navigation logic

District navigation should follow the common conceptual order given above. Labels may vary by district, but the logic must remain recognizable.

## CUR-NAV-05 — Orientation is always available

At every meaningful depth, the visitor must be able to understand the current district, the current content type, the parent section/collection/series/territory/family/lineage when applicable, the active navigation mode or lens, the route back to overview or comprehensive access, and the relationship to the wider platform.

## CUR-NAV-06 — Canonical routes are stable

Routes must use stable, human-readable identities. Canonical routes must not be defined by dates, temporary statuses, technologies, visual formats, campaign labels, or presentation modes. Route changes require an explicit migration decision.

## CUR-NAV-07 — Position is preserved on return

Opening and closing an entry, focus state, comparison, exhibition, filtered view, or archive detail should preserve the visitor's prior position and active context whenever possible. Return must restore orientation rather than reset the experience arbitrarily.

## CUR-NAV-08 — Previous and next require real sequence

Previous and next controls may appear only when an intentional sequence exists: a series, an exhibition, a research sequence, a case-study chapter, an experiment lineage, or a deliberately ordered collection. They must not imply ranking or sequence among unrelated entries.

## CUR-NAV-09 — No essential hidden navigation

No essential route may depend on hover, animation, precise pointing, spatial interpretation, sound, gesture discovery, or a large viewport.

## CUR-NAV-10 — Search and Index use the canonical set

Search, Index, filtered views, curated selections, collections, timelines, and related-content passages must point to canonical records. They must not create competing copies or a second taxonomy.

## CUR-NAV-11 — Search results explain context

A search result should communicate, where relevant: title, content type, primary district, why it matched, current public state, date or period, principal classification, and meaningful relationships.

## CUR-NAV-12 — Direct exit remains available

Focused, immersive, transactional, and long-form states must provide clear and predictable exit, close, back, and return controls. The visitor must never become trapped in immersion.

## 7. Cross-District Relationship Requirements

### 7.1 Ownership matrix

| Domain | Canonical owner | Owns | Does not own |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entrance and orientation | Home | Entrance, shared world, platform orientation, district thresholds, common origin and return | Complete project, research, experiment, artwork, product, or biography records |
| Professional outcomes | Work | Resolved or substantial professional outcomes, project context, role, contribution, responsibility, method, outcome, credits, professional evidence | Experiments merely because they appear impressive |
| Structured understanding | Research | Questions, intellectual framing, sources, methods, evidence as interpreted knowledge, arguments, positions, research artifacts, development of understanding | Delivered outcomes; experimental process |
| Experimental record | Lab | Framed uncertainty, setup, transformation, observable evidence, versions, branches, failures, invalid tests, abandoned paths, lineages, experimental interpretation, open edges | The resolved outcome once matured elsewhere — but retains the experimental record regardless |
| Artwork and encounter | Art | The autonomous artwork, artistic identity, curatorial context, series and exhibition relationships, perceptual encounter, artwork-specific documentation distinctions | The commercial offer once an artwork becomes purchasable |
| Commercial offer | Products | Value proposition, offered form, price or quotation basis, availability, variants, edition availability, licensing summary, fulfilment, transaction, commercial conditions, product support, commercial lifecycle state | The canonical creative, professional, research, or experimental origin record |
| Authorship and continuity | About | Personal and professional identity, position, biography, trajectory, experience chronology, education, values, methods, languages, location/operating context, availability, credentials, practice-level relationships, organizational evolution, CV/résumé access, contact | Complete case studies, research papers, experiment records, artwork presentations, product listings, or transaction information |
| Platform-wide legal identity | Global legal and administrative utility | Seller legal identity, rights-holder identity, privacy policy, general terms, statutory disclosures, company or individual business identity | Offer-specific commercial terms (Products); personal/professional identity (About) |

### 7.2 Shared ownership rules

## CUR-OWN-01 — One canonical record

Every substantive item has exactly one canonical record and one primary district. Selections, collections, series, exhibitions, timelines, filters, Search, Index, and cross-district passages reference that record.

## CUR-OWN-02 — Home owns orientation

Home owns entrance, shared world, platform orientation, district thresholds, and common origin and return. Home does not own complete project, research, experiment, artwork, product, or biography records.

## CUR-OWN-03 — Work owns resolved outcomes

Work owns professional or substantial outcomes, project context, role, contribution, responsibility, method, outcome, credits, and professional evidence. Work does not absorb experiments merely because they appear impressive.

## CUR-OWN-04 — Research owns structured understanding

Research owns questions, intellectual framing, sources, methods, evidence as interpreted knowledge, arguments, positions, research artifacts, and the development of understanding.

## CUR-OWN-05 — Lab owns the experimental record

Lab owns framed uncertainty, setup, transformation, observable evidence, versions, branches, failures, invalid tests, abandoned paths, lineages, experimental interpretation, and open edges. When an outcome matures elsewhere, Lab retains the experimental record.

## CUR-OWN-06 — Art owns the artwork and encounter

Art owns the autonomous artwork, artistic identity, curatorial context, series and exhibition relationships, perceptual encounter, and artwork-specific documentation distinctions. Artwork, event, documentation, reconstruction, and commercial edition must not be conflated.

## CUR-OWN-07 — Products owns the offer

Products owns value proposition, offered form, price or quotation basis, availability, variants, edition availability, licensing summary, fulfilment, transaction, commercial conditions, product support, and commercial lifecycle state. Products does not replace the canonical creative, professional, research, or experimental origin record.

## CUR-OWN-08 — About owns authorship and continuity

About owns personal and professional identity, position, biography, trajectory, experience chronology, education, values, methods, languages, location or operating context, availability, credentials, practice-level relationships, organizational evolution, CV and résumé access, and contact. About does not own complete case studies, research papers, experiment records, artwork presentations, product listings, or transaction information.

## CUR-OWN-09 — Global utility owns platform legal identity

The global legal and administrative utility owns seller legal identity, rights-holder identity, privacy policy, general terms, statutory disclosures, and company or individual business identity. Products owns offer-specific commercial terms. About owns personal and professional identity. (Full detail in §17.)

## CUR-OWN-10 — Collections do not own entries

Collections, exhibitions, constellations, practice areas, territories, themes, families, and curated displays organize or interpret content. They do not create duplicate canonical identities.

## CUR-OWN-11 — Maturity does not erase history

When content evolves, meaningful earlier stages remain traceable, experimental history is not rewritten as inevitable progress, transferred or mature records link back to origin, and a new canonical record is created only when the later state has independent visitor value and a distinct ownership purpose.

## CUR-OWN-12 — Archives preserve reachable history

Archived, unavailable, sold, discontinued, paused, failed, or transferred records remain reachable when they retain documentary value, provenance, compatibility relevance, support relevance, intellectual lineage, or external references. Their inactive state must be explicit.

### 7.3 Cross-district transitions

## CUR-TRN-01 — Home to district

A district entrance must identify the destination, suggest its difference, preserve continuity with the shared world, remain immediately accessible, and avoid becoming a miniature simulation of the district. At least one trace of direction, alignment, light, horizon, framing, or rhythm should survive the passage.

## CUR-TRN-02 — District to Home

Returning to Home must restore common orientation. It should preserve a trace of the district visited or the visitor's prior position when appropriate, without turning Home into a history dashboard.

## CUR-TRN-03 — District to district

A direct cross-district transition is permitted only when a real relationship exists. The link must state destination district, relationship type, and what the destination owns. It must not behave as a generic recommendation carousel.

## CUR-TRN-04 — Research and Lab are cyclical

Research may generate a Lab experiment. Lab evidence may revise, limit, or reopen a Research question. The system must support both directions without implying a mandatory linear pipeline.

## CUR-TRN-05 — Lab transfer preserves process

When a Lab outcome matures into Work, Products, Art, or Research: the Lab record remains available, the destination receives its own canonical record when appropriate, the transfer is labeled, and experimental uncertainty is not rewritten as resolved history.

## CUR-TRN-06 — Work and Research preserve ownership

Work may link to deeper research foundations. Research may link to professional application. Work owns the outcome and contribution. Research owns the inquiry and understanding.

## CUR-TRN-07 — Art and Products remain distinct

Art owns the artwork and encounter. Products owns acquisition, price, edition availability, license, fulfilment, and transaction. Commercial controls must remain secondary within Art. Encounter precedes transaction.

## CUR-TRN-08 — Art and Lab distinguish outcome from process

Lab owns experimental development and evidence. Art owns the autonomous artistic encounter. A generative or computational process does not determine ownership by itself.

## CUR-TRN-09 — About provides authorship, not duplication

Districts may link to About for biography, position, experience, values, practice context, availability, or contact. About must not duplicate the detailed records owned by those districts.

## CUR-TRN-10 — Focus and return preserve memory

Moving from field, exhibition, archive, catalogue, lineage, or timeline into focus must preserve the route back to the same context.

## 8. Shared Content and Information Requirements

### 8.1 Universal obligations

Every substantive canonical record should support, where applicable:

- canonical record identity;
- content type;
- title;
- public state;
- authorship or contribution;
- credits;
- date or period;
- relationships to other canonical records;
- version, where the record has meaningful states over time;
- last updated;
- archive state;
- current versus historical condition;
- public versus latent/private layer;
- an essential layer (identity, state, primary content) distinguishable from an extended layer (deeper narrative, process, context);
- support for both direct evaluation and deeper exploration of the same record.

This is an information obligation, not a page template or a database schema — how these fields are gathered, labeled, and displayed remains a Design System and Platform Specification decision.

### 8.2 District-specific obligations

Fields beyond the universal set — a Work entry's role/contribution/method sequence, a Research artifact's question/evidence/interpretation layers, a Lab experiment's setup/transformation/observation structure, an Art entry's medium/dimensions/series relationship, a Products entry's price/availability/fulfilment sequence, an About record's role/period/context structure — remain owned and defined by that district's own document. This document does not duplicate them; it only requires that the universal layer above sits beneath all of them consistently.

### 8.3 Shared structural terms

**Platform** — the complete Personal Platform. **District** — one of the seven major visitor-intention environments. **Entry** — a substantive canonical content record. **Subentry** — an independently useful unit within an entry, introduced only when substantial enough to justify independent navigation. **Curated layer** — an edited reading that communicates significance. **Comprehensive layer** — the complete public archive, catalogue, or Index. **Collection** — a curated grouping that references canonical entries without owning them. **Index** — the complete, precise, non-curated public view.

### 8.4 Controlled vocabulary rule

Public classifications and labels must use controlled vocabulary. The system must avoid uncontrolled tag clouds, synonyms with identical meaning, decorative classification, internal workflow language exposed publicly without purpose, and labels understandable only to the project team.

## 9. Hierarchy and Progressive Disclosure

### 9.1 Shared district pattern

Most districts use a common sequence, recomposed per district but conceptually recognizable throughout:

1. Threshold or entrance;
2. orientation;
3. curated or current foreground;
4. relational field, sequence, family, territory, or grouping;
5. archive, catalogue, or Index depth;
6. meaningful cross-district passages;
7. return to the wider universe.

### 9.2 Disclosure rules

Essential information comes before extended narrative. Progressive disclosure reduces density; it does not conceal critical information. Professional evaluation must remain fast. Exploratory depth remains available for those who want it.

Progressive disclosure must never conceal: status, authorship, contribution, uncertainty, experimental conditions, price, availability, limitations, acquisition consequences, support expectations, accessibility requirements, or exit controls.

## 10. Interaction Requirements

## CUR-INT-01 — Interaction has a defined purpose

Interaction may reveal, orient, distinguish, select, compare, connect, trace, confirm, or return. Interaction must not seek attention for its own sake.

## CUR-INT-02 — Interaction is reversible

Filters, lenses, focus states, comparisons, expansions, and navigation changes must be reversible. The current state and the route back must remain understandable.

## CUR-INT-03 — Progressive disclosure does not conceal essentials

See §9.2.

## CUR-INT-04 — Focus reduces unrelated signals

Selecting an entry or item should bring its identity, state, evidence, content, or media into clarity while unrelated material recedes. Focus must preserve position, parent context, return path, and essential controls.

## CUR-INT-05 — Filters recompose intentionally

Changing a filter, lens, territory, family, medium, status, or collection should produce an intentional composition. The interface must not merely hide items and leave arbitrary gaps. Active filters and result scope must be visible and resettable.

## CUR-INT-06 — Comparison is explicit

Where comparison is meaningful, the system should align stable dimensions and explain what is being compared. Comparison must not manufacture differences or imply equivalence where none exists.

## CUR-INT-07 — State changes are explicit

Loading, empty, no-result, unavailable, archived, paused, failed, invalid, transferred, discontinued, and confirmation states must be named directly. State must not be communicated through styling alone.

## CUR-INT-08 — Loading preserves structure

Media and content frames must retain their expected proportions during loading. Layouts must not jump. Placeholders must not imitate completed projects, artworks, products, evidence, or content that does not exist.

## CUR-INT-09 — Motion never gates access

See §11.

## CUR-INT-10 — Sound requires intent

Sound is non-essential and off by default. Media containing sound requires explicit visitor activation and must provide appropriate control, duration information, and transcript or equivalent when applicable.

## CUR-INT-11 — Commitment labels are literal

Commercial and contact actions must name the real commitment: purchase, reserve, subscribe, request a quote, apply for a commission, book a consultation, send a message. Generic labels must not conceal the action's consequence.

### 10.1 Global versus district-specific interaction

The eleven laws above apply everywhere. How they are expressed — Lab's comparison of versions, Art's focused-encounter recession, Products' evaluation-to-commitment flow, Research's progressive layering of question/context/evidence — remains each district's own pattern, built from these shared laws rather than replacing them.

## 11. Motion and Transition Requirements

Exact durations and easing are explicitly out of scope here — see §21 and §25.

### 11.1 Approved motion roles

threshold · passage · approach · focus · sequence · comparison · assembly · trace · recession · return · confirmation · state change

### 11.2 Prohibited motion roles

spectacle · forced cinematic introduction · ambient noise · false activity · manufactured urgency · decorative particles · gamified navigation · transition delay · hidden loading · disorienting camera behavior

### 11.3 Reduced-motion obligations

Meaning must survive without motion. Every motion category in §11.1 must have a reduced-motion equivalent that preserves meaning through immediate state change, opacity or emphasis change, static alignment, explicit labels, or preserved position.

### 11.4 Additional rules

- Motion preserves spatial memory (origin, direction, parent context, selected item, active lens, return position).
- District transitions preserve continuity — see CUR-TRN-01 through CUR-TRN-03.
- Artwork motion remains distinct from interface motion (Art §22 and §28 of `ART_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md`).
- Repeated navigation remains efficient — motion must not become ceremony on every visit.
- Focused modes have direct exit at all times (CUR-NAV-12).
- Commercial motion never creates false scarcity or urgency (Products §6, §17 below).

**CUR-INT-09 — Motion never gates access:** Motion may clarify hierarchy, direction, relationship, assembly, focus, sequence, trace, or return. It must not delay access, force a journey, create interaction traps, replace labels, imply activity that is not real, or become the main source of district identity.

## 12. Responsive Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Recomposition, not shrinking | Responsive behavior must preserve hierarchy and relationships through recomposition, not proportional shrinking |
| Spatial distance becomes ordered sequence | Wide-layout distance, adjacency, or territory becomes deliberate vertical order, grouping, labeling, or sequence on small screens |
| Hierarchy survives | Primary/secondary/tertiary emphasis remains legible at every breakpoint |
| Parallel structures become legible linear structures | Multi-track timelines, side-by-side comparisons, and parallel fields become ordered sequences, not truncated |
| No essential content depends on wide-screen spatiality | Every essential relationship has a non-spatial equivalent |
| Mobile is not one generic card stack | Meaningful variation in presentation survives responsive recomposition |
| Touch usability | Targets remain usable without precision pointing |
| Explicit state and relationships | State and relationship information does not disappear on small screens |
| District identity survives mobile transformation | A district recomposed for mobile is still recognizably that district |
| Media integrity | Aspect ratio, legibility, captions, credits, controls, and color/proportion fidelity survive responsive transformation; a universal crop is prohibited |
| Non-hover equivalence | Every hover-dependent enhancement has a touch/keyboard equivalent |
| Alternate representations for complex diagrams | Timelines, maps, fields, lineages, and relationship diagrams provide readable list, table, sequence, or text alternatives on constrained screens |

## CUR-RSP-01 — Recompose rather than mechanically stack

Responsive behavior must preserve hierarchy and relationships through recomposition. Desktop spatial arrangements must not become repetitive card stacks by default.

## CUR-RSP-02 — Spatial distance becomes ordered sequence

Where wide layouts communicate distance, adjacency, or territory, small screens should translate those relationships into a deliberate vertical order, grouping, labeling, or sequence.

## CUR-RSP-03 — Curatorial relationships survive

Series, comparisons, project evidence, lineages, product choices, and timeline relationships must remain legible across viewports. Responsive behavior must not destroy meaningful pairing or order.

## CUR-RSP-04 — Media retains integrity

Responsive media must preserve aspect ratio, legibility, captions, credits, controls, artwork color and proportion, and drawing or diagram readability. A universal crop is prohibited.

## CUR-RSP-05 — Navigation remains direct

Primary navigation, district navigation, Search, Index, Contact, return, and active-context controls must remain directly accessible on small screens.

## CUR-RSP-06 — Density changes without information loss

Secondary information may move behind progressive disclosure, but essential identity, status, navigation, evidence, and commercial conditions must remain available.

## CUR-RSP-07 — Mobile has intentional rhythm

Mobile must preserve changes in scale, pacing, emphasis, and pause. It should read as an architectural promenade or editorial sequence rather than a monotonous list.

## CUR-RSP-08 — Hover enhancements disappear safely

Any hover enhancement must have a touch, keyboard, and non-hover equivalent. No information or action may disappear when hover is unavailable.

## CUR-RSP-09 — Complex diagrams receive alternate views

Timelines, maps, fields, lineages, and relationship diagrams must provide readable list, table, sequence, or text alternatives on constrained screens.

## 13. Accessibility Requirements

> **Accessibility is part of the primary system, not a fallback version.**

## CUR-ACC-01 — Spatial views require semantic equivalents

Every spatial, atmospheric, map-like, lineage, timeline, exhibition, or field view must have a structured semantic equivalent. Accessibility is part of the primary system, not a fallback site.

## CUR-ACC-02 — Relationships must be machine-readable

Connections communicated through position, lines, proximity, or motion must also be expressed semantically and textually.

## CUR-ACC-03 — Complete keyboard access

All navigation, controls, focus states, filters, media, forms, comparisons, and immersive modes must be operable by keyboard.

## CUR-ACC-04 — Visible focus

Keyboard focus must remain clearly visible in every district and on every surface. District atmosphere must not suppress focus visibility.

## CUR-ACC-05 — Touch access

Touch targets must remain usable on small screens. Essential interaction must not require precision pointing.

## CUR-ACC-06 — Readable contrast and typography

Text, controls, labels, metadata, annotations, and focus indicators must maintain sufficient contrast. Typography must remain scalable and readable over atmospheric backgrounds and media.

## CUR-ACC-07 — No single-channel meaning

Essential meaning must not be communicated by color alone, motion alone, position alone, hover alone, sound alone, or shape alone.

## CUR-ACC-08 — Reduced motion equivalence

Reduced-motion mode must preserve hierarchy, relationship, orientation, state change, focus, return, and access to all content. It must not simply remove movement and leave missing meaning.

## CUR-ACC-09 — Media alternatives

The system must support, where applicable: meaningful alternative text, captions, transcripts, audio description or equivalent description, duration, playback control, pause and stop, and identification of documentation versus the work itself. Alternative text must describe what is present without inventing unsupported interpretation.

## CUR-ACC-10 — Predictable history and exit

Browser back behavior, close controls, and return controls must behave predictably, especially in focused or immersive states.

## CUR-ACC-11 — Core information requires no zoom

Zoom must not be required to read core text, status, metadata, controls, or commercial conditions. High-resolution detail may remain optional for media inspection.

## CUR-ACC-12 — Forms remain low-friction and explicit

Forms must use clear labels, required-field indication, understandable errors, preserved input where safe, and explicit success or failure confirmation. Contact must not require unnecessary personal information.

## CUR-ACC-13 — Performance supports access

The experience must remain usable on modest devices and slower connections. Deliberate pacing must come from composition and transition, not slow loading.

## CUR-ACC-14 — Language state is explicit

When multiple languages are activated: conceptual hierarchy remains identical, missing translations are stated, an incomplete translation must not silently replace an available original, and language must not create a separate district structure.

## 14. Shared State and Lifecycle Requirements

District lifecycles are not collapsed into one global list — they remain separately owned, but every one of them must obey the same shared obligations:

- every public state is explicit;
- state describes real condition;
- state is not implied only through style;
- current and historical states remain distinguishable;
- transfer does not erase origin;
- archive does not mean deletion;
- unavailable does not always mean invisible;
- failed, paused, abandoned, discontinued, sold, superseded, and archived states require truthful explanation;
- public state and internal workflow status remain separate.

| District/system | Lifecycle vocabulary owner | Examples | Shared obligation |

### 14.1 Global interface states

The interface must support: loading, empty, sparse, no results, unavailable, archived, error, success or confirmation, and restricted or unpublished (when applicable).

### 14.2 Public content states (from Information Architecture)

Ongoing, Completed, Archived, Available, Unavailable, Edition Closed — appearing only when meaningful to the content type.

### 14.3 Editorial-state honesty

The interface must not make hypotheses look verified, prototypes look market-ready, failed tests look successful, unavailable products look purchasable, documentation look identical to an artwork, collaborative work look solely authored, or archived content look current.

## 15. Search, Index, and Discovery Requirements

> **Search and Index are precise counterparts to spatial exploration.**

Global Search and the global Index sit alongside district-level indexes and filters as core, non-optional utilities (CUR-NAV-03). Requirements:

- filters remain visible while active, individually removable, and directly resettable;
- result count or scope is shown where relevant;
- results explain *why* they matched, not only that they matched (CUR-NAV-11);
- archived results may appear, but their status is unmistakable;
- an empty result state explains the active conditions and offers a route back to the complete set, never a dead end;
- small collections receive proportionate, non-inflated presentation rather than simulated scale;
- filter complexity scales with actual content volume — a small catalogue does not imitate a large marketplace (`PRODUCTS_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §16);
- browsing position is preserved when a visitor returns from an opened result (CUR-NAV-07);
- results always point to canonical records — never a duplicate taxonomy (CUR-NAV-10);
- each result indicates district, content type, and state, so a visitor can judge relevance before opening it.

## 16. Archive and Historical Continuity Requirements

- Superseded content is archived, not silently erased.
- Canonical records preserve meaningful history rather than presenting only their final state.
- Archival visibility depends on documentary, provenance, compatibility, support, intellectual, or external-reference value (CUR-OWN-12).
- The archive does not compete with current orientation — it is reachable, not foregrounded (CUR-SPA-06).
- Current, superseded, inactive, and historical states are explicit, never implied.
- Earlier versions remain traceable; links and lineage remain preserved across transitions.
- Transferred content retains its origin history (CUR-TRN-05, CUR-OWN-11).
- Sold or discontinued items may remain visible where appropriate, clearly marked (`PRODUCTS_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §13).
- Failed and abandoned work is preserved where meaningful, with the same editorial dignity as successful work (`LAB_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §15).

This section governs the *behavior* archived content must exhibit on the platform. It is not repository-management instruction — how `docs/archive/` itself is organized is a project-tracking concern, already handled in `docs/archive/universes/ARCHIVE_INDEX.md`, and out of scope here.

## 18. Shared Tone and Editorial Requirements

Shared baseline, drawn from and consistent with `RESEARCH_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §11 (editorial language), `ABOUT_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §6 (tone), `LAB_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §21 (tone of voice), and `PRODUCTS_UNIVERSE_v1.1.md` §6 (commerce without pressure):

- precise;
- restrained;
- direct;
- evidence-aware;
- honest about state;
- clear about uncertainty;
- professionally legible;
- free from marketing pressure.

**Prohibited platform-wide:**

- inflated claims;
- generic innovation language;
- unexplained jargon;
- false certainty;
- manufactured authority;
- personal mythology;
- romanticization of failure;
- ambiguity where professional, experimental, legal, or commercial clarity is required;
- false scarcity;
- manipulative conversion language.

**Shared baseline versus district voice:** this baseline is a floor, not a uniform. Research's editorial rigor, Lab's candor about failure, About's first-person restraint, and Products' commercial plainness are the *same* baseline applied to different subject matter — not four different voices. A district may develop its own emphasis; none may drop below this floor.

### 23.1 Implementation acceptance criteria

4. Direct access remains available alongside spatial exploration.
5. Essential navigation works without hover, animation, sound, or large-screen spatial interpretation.
6. Deep pages preserve orientation.
7. Returning from focus or detail restores meaningful context.
8. Cross-district links state destination and relationship.
9. Atmosphere never obscures evidence, status, price, attribution, or action.
10. Spatial views have semantic and responsive equivalents.
11. Keyboard, touch, focus, contrast, reduced motion, and media alternatives are implemented.
12. Responsive layouts preserve hierarchy and relationships rather than mechanically stacking cards.

14. District-specific lifecycle states remain distinct from project-management statuses.
15. Lab history remains visible after transfer.
16. Art remains canonical in Art when commercialized.
17. Products owns the offer but not the origin record.
18. About provides authorship and contact without duplicating district evidence.
19. Global legal and administrative information remains separate from About and offer-specific Product content.

## 27. Final Definition

This document translates seven distinct universe specifications into one shared system of obligations. Coherence does not require sameness: Home's openness, Work's density, Research's diffusion, Lab's instrumentation, Art's silence, Products' clarity, and About's intimacy remain seven different expressions of the same small set of laws — one navigational model, one accessibility floor, one ownership discipline, one editorial baseline — applied at seven different degrees. District identity remains protected precisely because the shared structure beneath it is fixed. Future design and implementation decisions — the Design System, the Animation System, the Platform Specification, and everything built on top of them — must satisfy every requirement recorded here.

**The cross-universe system is the common structure that allows every district to remain distinct without becoming disconnected.**
