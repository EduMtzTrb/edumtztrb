# Personal Platform — Research Universe

## Document Status

**Status:** Proposal

**Output:** `RESEARCH_UNIVERSE.md`  
**Scope:** Identity, atmosphere, homepage, navigation, layouts, interactions, and future research organization  
**Excluded:** Individual papers, thesis arguments, research findings, and project-specific content

---

# 1. Purpose

The Research Universe is the part of the Personal Platform dedicated to inquiry.

It is not simply a list of papers, academic credentials, or thesis documents. It is a spatial representation of an evolving intellectual practice: the questions being investigated, the territories being connected, the methods being developed, and the ideas that remain open.

Within the complete platform:

- **Work** presents resolved or professionally delivered outcomes.
- **Lab** presents experiments, prototypes, and tests.
- **Research** presents questions, frameworks, evidence, interpretations, and evolving bodies of knowledge.

The Research Universe should communicate that research is an active design practice rather than a static archive.

---

# 2. Core Identity

## 2.1 Proposed identity

The Research Universe is conceived as an **architectural observatory and living archive**.

The observatory represents looking outward: identifying questions, patterns, unknowns, and future directions.

The archive represents looking inward: organizing sources, notes, methods, arguments, and accumulated knowledge.

The combination creates a district that feels simultaneously:

- analytical
- contemplative
- precise
- unfinished
- interconnected
- expandable

## 2.2 Central idea

> Research is not displayed as a shelf of finished documents. It is revealed as a field of connected questions.

## 2.3 Desired perception

A visitor entering the Research Universe should understand that:

1. The research belongs to the same design practice as the architecture, software, AI, and art elsewhere in the platform.
2. The work is rigorous without feeling institutionally generic.
3. Ideas remain traceable as they evolve.
4. Finished publications are only one visible layer of a larger research process.
5. The universe can expand from a small initial collection into a substantial long-term knowledge system.

# 3. Relationship to the Shared Metaverse

The Research Universe remains part of the platform's common architectural world.

It inherits the shared principles of:

- minimalism
- line-work geometry
- spatial hierarchy
- calm pacing
- restrained motion
- architectural proportion
- exploratory navigation

Its distinct identity should emerge through the way these shared elements are used rather than through an unrelated visual style.

## 3.1 Shared language

The district should continue using the platform's common language of:

- plans
- sections
- axes
- grids
- boundaries
- coordinates
- thresholds
- voids
- fields
- connections

## 3.2 Research-specific interpretation

In the Research Universe, architectural line work becomes a system for representing knowledge:

- axes become lines of inquiry
- nodes become questions or research bodies
- boundaries become domains
- layers become levels of evidence or development
- annotations become metadata
- paths become conceptual relationships
- voids become unresolved areas
- coordinates become navigational references

This makes the Research Universe recognizable as part of the platform while giving it its own intellectual atmosphere.

# 4. Atmosphere

## 4.1 Spatial character

The Research Universe should feel like entering a quiet, expansive room whose full limits are not immediately visible.

It should evoke:

- a reading room after hours
- a scientific observatory
- an unfinished drawing table
- a cartographic archive
- a field of faint conceptual traces

The space should feel inhabited by thought, but not cluttered by information.

## 4.2 Emotional tone

The atmosphere should be:

- calm rather than dramatic
- curious rather than authoritative
- rigorous rather than academic-looking
- mysterious without becoming obscure
- incomplete without appearing disorganized
- dense in meaning but sparse in presentation

## 4.3 Light and depth

The Home Universe may establish the general environmental lighting. Within Research, the light should become more diffuse and analytical.

Suggested qualities:

- low-contrast ambient depth
- thin illuminated lines
- subtle layers receding into space
- isolated areas of sharper focus
- large quiet fields around active information

Exact colors, typography, shadows, and effects remain governed by the shared Design System.

## 4.4 Visual density

The district should never display the entire archive at equal intensity.

Information should appear in layers:

1. **Orientation layer** — where the visitor is.
2. **Territory layer** — the major research areas.
3. **Relationship layer** — how areas connect.
4. **Detail layer** — metadata and descriptions.
5. **Archive layer** — the complete structured index.

This progressive disclosure preserves calmness as the research collection grows.

# 5. Experience Narrative

The Research homepage should function as a threshold into a knowledge landscape.

## 5.1 Arrival

The visitor enters a sparse field with a clear title and a short statement defining the role of Research within the wider practice.

The first impression should not be a conventional feed or publication list. It should be an overview of the intellectual territory.

## 5.2 Orientation

The visitor sees several research territories positioned within a larger field. Their arrangement suggests relationships without requiring prior knowledge.

Each territory should communicate:

- its name
- its current state
- its relative scale or activity
- its relationship to adjacent territories

## 5.3 Exploration

The visitor can approach a territory and reveal its internal structure. This may expose research lines, recurring questions, methods, outputs, and connected experiments.

The interface should reward curiosity but never require hidden gestures to access essential information.

## 5.4 Focus

Selecting a territory transitions from the broad field into a quieter, more legible research overview.

The visitor moves from:

**universe → territory → research line → artifact**

This hierarchy should remain understandable at every depth.

## 5.5 Return

Returning to the Research homepage should preserve orientation. The visitor should understand where the selected item belonged within the larger field.

# 6. Research Homepage

## 6.1 Homepage role

The Research homepage must perform five functions:

1. Establish the identity of the Research Universe.
2. Present the current research territories.
3. Reveal relationships between those territories.
4. Provide direct access to a complete textual index.
5. Remain scalable as the archive grows.

## 6.2 Proposed homepage sequence

### Zone A — Threshold

The first viewport introduces the district.

Content:

- label: `Research`
- concise identity statement
- current orientation marker
- optional indication of active research activity
- entry controls for `Explore`, `Index`, and `Search`

The threshold should use minimal text and substantial spatial breathing room.

### Zone B — Research Field

The primary homepage element is a navigable field of research territories.

This field may resemble:

- a plan
- a constellation
- a diagrammatic site
- an annotated conceptual map

It should not resemble a decorative network visualization. Every visible relationship must have a meaningful organizational purpose.

Each territory is represented by a stable spatial anchor. New territories can be added without redesigning the entire composition.

### Zone C — Current Lines of Inquiry

A focused area presents the research lines currently receiving attention.

This is not a news feed. It is a curated view into the active edge of the archive.

Possible information:

- research line title
- short framing question
- status
- last meaningful update
- related territory

The number of visible items should remain deliberately limited.

### Zone D — Archive Entrance

A transition leads from spatial exploration to systematic browsing.

The archive entrance should offer:

- complete index
- filters
- chronology
- research types
- status views
- connected outputs

This allows visitors who prefer direct navigation to bypass exploratory interaction.

### Zone E — Cross-Universe Connections

A restrained section shows where research connects to other districts.

Examples of relationship types:

- research informing professional work
- research becoming a Lab experiment
- research producing a tool or product
- research expressed through art

The section should show relationships, not duplicate content owned by other universes.

### Zone F — Exit and Wider Platform

The page concludes with clear routes back to the Home Universe and toward adjacent districts, especially Work and Lab.

# 7. Navigation System

The Research Universe should provide two parallel navigation modes.

## 7.1 Spatial mode

Spatial mode is the atmospheric default.

It allows visitors to explore research through territories, proximity, paths, and relationships.

Appropriate for:

- first-time visitors
- discovery
- understanding the research practice as a whole
- seeing conceptual relationships

## 7.2 Index mode

Index mode is the precise textual alternative.

It allows visitors to browse and filter all available research entities without relying on spatial interaction.

Appropriate for:

- returning visitors
- academic or professional reviewers
- users seeking a specific item
- accessibility
- large archives

Neither mode is secondary. They are two views of the same structured content.

## 7.3 Persistent local navigation

The Research Universe should maintain a stable local navigation layer containing:

- `Overview`
- `Territories`
- `Index`
- `Chronology`
- `Search`

These labels are proposals and should be coordinated with the Information Architecture before approval.

## 7.4 Global navigation

The global platform navigation must remain accessible without overpowering the local Research experience.

The visitor should always be able to identify:

- the current universe
- the current depth
- the route back to Research
- the route back to Home

## 7.5 Breadcrumb model

Deep pages should use a visible hierarchy such as:

`Research / Territory / Research Line / Artifact`

The breadcrumb should operate as both navigation and conceptual orientation.

# 8. Layout System

## 8.1 General layout principle

Research layouts should resemble structured working surfaces rather than conventional editorial templates.

The main compositional qualities are:

- a strong underlying grid
- large empty areas
- asymmetric but controlled balance
- precise annotations
- layered information
- clear reading paths

## 8.2 Homepage layout

The homepage should prioritize the research field over a sequence of cards.

On wide screens:

- the field may occupy most of the viewport
- local navigation may sit along a controlled edge
- contextual information may appear in an adjacent annotation panel
- connections may extend across the composition

On smaller screens:

- territories become a deliberate vertical sequence
- relationships are expressed through indentation, labels, and connectors
- the textual hierarchy replaces spatial distance where needed
- all core content remains available without horizontal exploration

## 8.3 Territory overview layout

A territory page should contain:

1. territory identity
2. framing description
3. active research lines
4. relationship map
5. associated methods or recurring concepts
6. outputs and artifacts
7. chronology or development history
8. connected universes

The page should reveal a coherent body of inquiry rather than a project gallery.

## 8.4 Research line layout

Future research-line pages should support:

- central question
- scope
- current state
- related questions
- methods
- sources
- notes
- outputs
- development history

This document defines only the structural capacity for those elements. It does not define their content.

## 8.5 Archive layout

The archive should prioritize clarity and scanning.

It may combine:

- a compact table or list
- filters
- a chronological axis
- relationship indicators
- saved or shareable views in future versions

The archive should remain visually connected to the spatial universe through shared lines, coordinates, typography, and annotations.

# 9. Interaction Philosophy

## 9.1 Research interactions should reveal structure

Interaction must help visitors understand relationships, states, and hierarchy.

It should not exist merely to make the page feel futuristic.

## 9.2 Progressive disclosure

At rest, the interface shows only essential orientation.

As the visitor focuses on an element, it may reveal:

- a short description
- current status
- associated questions
- related territories
- connected outputs
- temporal information

## 9.3 Focus and relation

When a territory or research line receives focus:

- unrelated elements recede slightly
- connected elements become more legible
- the selected element remains anchored
- a concise information layer appears

This should produce clarity rather than spectacle.

## 9.4 Movement

Transitions should feel like moving between scales of a drawing:

- overview
- area
- detail
- annotation

Useful motion metaphors include:

- controlled zoom
- planar translation
- line extension
- layer separation
- focus adjustment

Avoid:

- uncontrolled camera movement
- ornamental particles
- rapid parallax
- disorienting 3D rotation
- motion that delays access to content

## 9.5 Hover independence

Hover may enrich the desktop experience, but no essential information should depend on it.

Equivalent focus, tap, and keyboard behavior must exist.

## 9.6 Reduced motion

The reduced-motion experience should preserve hierarchy and meaning through static changes in emphasis rather than spatial animation.

## 9.7 Search behavior

Search should function as a research instrument rather than a generic site search.

Future search may expose:

- matching territories
- questions
- research lines
- people
- methods
- sources
- outputs
- tags

Results should indicate why each item matched and where it belongs.

# 10. Research Organization Framework

The universe requires a content model capable of supporting future growth. The following entities are proposals for coordination with the Information Architecture.

## 10.1 Primary entities

### Territory

A broad and durable area of research.

A territory should remain stable enough to organize multiple research lines over time.

### Research Line

A sustained inquiry within a territory.

It may contain several questions, methods, studies, outputs, and periods of activity.

### Question

A specific unresolved or guiding inquiry.

Questions may connect multiple research lines and territories.

### Artifact

A concrete research output or working object.

Possible artifact categories may eventually include:

- publication
- essay
- presentation
- dataset
- diagram
- bibliography
- method
- prototype
- lecture
- note collection

### Source

A referenced external work, author, case, archive, dataset, or precedent.

### Method

A recurring way of investigating, analyzing, comparing, documenting, or testing.

### Connection

An explicit relationship between research entities or between Research and another platform universe.

## 10.2 Supporting metadata

Potential metadata includes:

- title
- concise description
- territory
- research line
- status
- date created
- date updated
- period of activity
- artifact type
- methods
- collaborators
- institutions
- keywords
- related entities
- external publication state
- connected universe

Metadata should be structured from the beginning, even when the visible interface remains minimal.

## 10.3 Proposed research lifecycle

Research-specific lifecycle labels may include:

- **Seed** — an initial question or possible direction
- **Active** — currently being investigated
- **Paused** — intentionally inactive but still relevant
- **Developed** — sufficiently formed to produce stable outputs
- **Published** — publicly released through a formal artifact
- **Archived** — retained for reference but no longer active

These labels describe research activity. They do not replace the project-wide decision statuses of Idea, Proposal, Approved, Implemented, Verified, and Deprecated.

## 10.4 Organizational dimensions

The archive should eventually support browsing by:

- territory
- research line
- question
- artifact type
- method
- status
- chronology
- collaborator
- institution
- keyword
- connected universe

The interface should not expose all dimensions simultaneously. Filters should appear according to context.

# 11. Relationship with Other Universes

## 11.1 Research and Work

Research may explain the intellectual or methodological foundations of professional work.

The Work Universe should own the project presentation. Research should only show the relationship and the relevant research context.

## 11.2 Research and Lab

Research asks and frames questions. Lab tests possible answers.

A research line may generate several experiments. A Lab experiment may also generate new research questions.

This two-way relationship should be visible.

## 11.3 Research and Art

Art may translate research into perceptual, critical, or speculative forms.

Research should record the connection without absorbing the artwork into an academic structure.

## 11.4 Research and Products

Research may produce methods, tools, datasets, publications, or systems that later become products.

The Products Universe owns the commercial or distributable object. Research preserves its intellectual lineage.

## 11.5 Research and About

The About Universe may summarize research interests and experience. The Research Universe contains the detailed structure of the practice.

---

# 12. Scalability

## 12.1 Initial state

The Research Universe must remain convincing with only a small number of territories and research lines.

The initial version should not simulate a large archive that does not yet exist.

Empty space should communicate capacity rather than incompleteness.

## 12.2 Growth state

As content expands, the system should support:

- additional territories
- denser relationships
- deeper research hierarchies
- new artifact types
- collaborative research
- institutional affiliations
- multilingual content
- published and unpublished layers
- historical snapshots

## 12.3 Long-term state

The Research Universe could eventually operate as a public knowledge environment containing years of accumulated inquiry.

The underlying organization should make it possible to introduce future features without redefining the universe, including:

- advanced search
- citation relationships
- public bibliographies
- research timelines
- downloadable datasets
- research collections
- reading pathways
- thematic exhibitions
- collaborative profiles
- external publication records

These are future capacities, not current implementation commitments.

## 12.4 Stable anchors

To avoid structural redesign, the following should remain stable:

- the hierarchy from universe to artifact
- the distinction between spatial and index navigation
- the use of territories as primary organizers
- explicit relationships between entities
- structured metadata
- clear ownership across universes

# 13. Accessibility and Legibility

The Research Universe must remain usable without depending on atmosphere, animation, or spatial visualization.

Required principles:

- every spatial view has a structured textual equivalent
- all relationships communicated visually are also available semantically
- keyboard focus is clearly visible
- typography remains readable over atmospheric backgrounds
- contrast is sufficient at every information layer
- animation can be reduced
- zoom is never required to read core information
- touch targets remain usable on small screens
- navigation does not rely on precision pointing
- statuses are not communicated by color alone

Accessibility should strengthen the dual-mode concept rather than be treated as a fallback.

# 14. Content and Editorial Principles

## 14.1 Curated, not exhaustive by default

The homepage should present a deliberate interpretation of the research practice. The archive provides completeness.

## 14.2 Questions before claims

Research entries should make the central inquiry visible before presenting outputs.

## 14.3 Process without clutter

The platform may expose development and uncertainty, but it should not publish every raw note indiscriminately.

## 14.4 Visible evolution

Where appropriate, research should show how a question, method, or position changed over time.

## 14.5 Clear states

Visitors should be able to distinguish:

- ongoing inquiry
- provisional material
- completed work
- formal publication
- archived material

## 14.6 No artificial authority

The visual language should not make preliminary research appear more resolved than it is.

---

# 15. Anti-Patterns

The Research Universe should not become:

- a generic academic profile
- a chronological blog
- a PDF repository
- a publication grid
- a decorative node network
- an imitation of scientific software
- a visually dense knowledge graph
- a thesis-only microsite
- a duplicate of the Lab Universe
- a collection of unrelated intellectual interests

It should avoid using complexity as evidence of rigor.

# 17. Experience Principles

The Research Universe should follow these principles:

1. **Make relationships legible.**  
   Visitors should understand how questions, territories, methods, and outputs connect.

2. **Preserve intellectual openness.**  
   Active research should not be presented as prematurely resolved.

3. **Provide two paths.**  
   Spatial exploration and precise indexing must coexist.

4. **Use atmosphere to support attention.**  
   The environment should slow the visitor down without obstructing access.

5. **Show only meaningful complexity.**  
   Every visible connection must represent an actual relationship.

6. **Separate inquiry from experimentation.**  
   Research and Lab remain connected but conceptually distinct.

7. **Scale through structure.**  
   Growth should add content and relationships, not require a new interface concept.

8. **Keep uncertainty organized.**  
   Openness should feel deliberate and traceable rather than unfinished by accident.

# 19. Proposed Decisions

The following decisions are proposed, not yet approved:

1. The Research Universe is framed as an **architectural observatory and living archive**.
2. Its primary homepage experience is a **field of connected research territories**.
3. It provides equal access through **spatial mode** and **index mode**.
4. Its stable hierarchy is **Universe → Territory → Research Line → Artifact**.
5. It visually distinguishes resolved outputs from active and provisional inquiry.
6. It treats relationships between entities as first-class content.
7. It remains structurally separate from Work and Lab while showing explicit connections to both.
8. It uses progressive disclosure to preserve calmness as the archive grows.
9. It supports a research lifecycle distinct from the platform's project decision statuses.
10. It launches honestly with a small collection and uses empty space as future capacity.

# 22. Summary

The Research Universe is a quiet architectural observatory and living archive within the Personal Platform.

Its purpose is to make an evolving intellectual practice visible: not only what has been published, but what is being questioned, connected, tested, developed, and preserved.

The homepage presents research as a field of territories rather than a conventional list. Visitors may explore this field spatially or use a complete structured index. As the archive grows, progressive disclosure, stable hierarchy, and explicit metadata prevent the experience from becoming visually or conceptually chaotic.

The result should feel rigorous, atmospheric, open, and structurally prepared for many years of research.
