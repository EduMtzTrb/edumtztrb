## 1. Purpose

**Products operationalizes value.**

The Products Universe is the commercial district — where resolved outcomes become offerable: purchasable, licensable, reservable, commissionable, subscribable, or otherwise exchangeable under explicit terms. It does not define the platform's identity. It provides the infrastructure through which selected parts of the practice enter exchange with others.

Work demonstrates capability. Research develops understanding. Lab tests and transforms. Art concentrates perception. **Products converts a sufficiently resolved outcome into a clear offer.**

The district functions simultaneously as showroom, catalogue, transaction environment, licensing interface, release record, and post-acquisition support threshold — coherent with one offer or a large catalogue.

---

## 2. The Role of Commerce Within the Platform

Commerce is a capability, not the platform's governing identity. The wider practice exists to represent one coherent design intelligence across architecture, computation, research, art, and experimentation; Products lets certain outcomes of that practice be exchanged without forcing every district to behave like a store.

The commercial layer stays visible without dominating, accessible without interrupting contemplation, and direct without becoming aggressive. **A visitor should be able to experience the entire platform without being treated primarily as a customer.** Once inside Products, the commercial conditions become explicit and dependable.

---

## 3. What Qualifies as a Product

Not defined by physical form or the presence of a price. A **product** is a resolved offer with: a defined recipient, a comprehensible value proposition, an identifiable deliverable or service outcome, explicit commercial conditions, sufficient stability to be represented truthfully, a bounded method of provision, a support or fulfilment path, and an accountable owner.

**Ownership tests against the neighbors** — each phrased as the two questions that actually distinguish the districts:

| District | That district asks | Products asks |
|---|---|---|
| Work | What was accomplished, and what capability does it demonstrate? | What can be obtained, under what terms, and what happens after? |
| Art | What is the work, and how should it be encountered? | In what form can it be acquired, licensed, or commissioned? |
| Lab | What is being tested, changed, or learned? | What stable value can be delivered now, under what conditions? |
| Research | What is being investigated or understood? | What usable form of that knowledge can be accessed? |

An experimental origin is not a defect in a product. Ambiguous operational status is.

**An outcome should not enter Products when:** its purpose can't be stated clearly, its deliverable is undefined, its limitations are materially unknown, or it would be safer and more truthful to present as Lab, Research, Work, or Art. Products must never be used to simulate commercial readiness.

---

## 4. When an Experiment Becomes Offerable

An offered state must be stable enough that another person can understand what they're receiving and reasonably rely on it — it does not need to be final in an absolute sense.

**The offerability test**, condensed to what each dimension is actually checking:

- **Value** — is the benefit and intended user understandable?
- **Definition** — is the offered version clearly bounded, with inclusions and exclusions stated?
- **Reliability** — does it perform consistently for its stated purpose, with limitations disclosed?
- **Provision** — can it be delivered as represented, with an owner responsible for fulfilment?
- **Commercial clarity** — are price, license, and commitment terms explicit?
- **Support** — is there a defined, truthful route for problems and updates?
- **Lifecycle** — can it be updated, paused, or retired without losing its history or confusing prior customers?

If these aren't met, the outcome stays in Lab or another canonical district. A product may still be experimental *as part of the offer* — beta, preview, pilot — provided the status is explicit and support expectations are bounded accordingly. These terms describe genuine operational distinctions, not promotional labels.

**Typical maturation paths** (illustrative, not mandatory):

```text
Research question → Lab experiment → validated version → defined offer → Product release
Art record → edition decision → Product offer
Work method → repeatable service → Product offer
```

The movement into Products creates a new commercial record. It never relocates or erases the canonical origin.

---

## 5. Core Spatial Concept — The Quiet Exchange Hall

Four spatial conditions: the **showroom** (selected offers, space and presence), the **catalogue archive** (the full body of offers, ordered and durable), the **evaluation table** (specifications and terms examined clearly), and the **counter** (commitment happens directly, without ambiguity).

Composed rather than crowded, exact rather than promotional. References — display bays, specimen tables, cataloguing marks, service counters — guide hierarchy and movement. The interface must never become a literal shop simulation.

---

## 6. Storefront Philosophy

**Curation before volume.** The homepage never attempts to expose the entire catalogue — it establishes the district and provides clear routes into it, working with one offer or a large catalogue without artificial filler.

**Understanding before commitment.** Every offer must let the visitor answer what it is, who it's for, what's included and excluded, how it's delivered, what it costs, and what happens next — no atmospheric treatment may obscure these answers.

**Commerce without pressure.** No false scarcity, invented countdowns, hidden fees, or obstructive cancellation. Real scarcity — edition limits, lead times, capacity — is stated plainly.

**Trust as architecture**, built into the information structure rather than added as reassurance near checkout: price, delivery method, cancellation terms, and seller identity should all be locatable before commitment, not discovered during it.

**Context without duplication.** A product record shows concise origin and links to its Work, Art, Research, or Lab source — it never duplicates that district's full narrative.

**Exchange, not merchandise.** The governing question is not "what can be sold?" It is: *which outcomes can be offered responsibly, and in what form does their value become transferable?*

---

## 7. Product Families

Approved structural capabilities — organizing offers by the nature of what is exchanged, not by marketing theme. Each requires its own record fields, detailed in the corresponding page type (§10):

- **Digital products** — files, software, data, or access; version, compatibility, license scope, update policy.
- **Physical objects** — dimensions, materials, fabrication method, shipping region, lead time.
- **Services** — purpose, scope, deliverables, process stages, availability, exclusions.
- **Licenses** — what's licensed, permitted and prohibited uses, term and seat limits.
- **Editions** — bounded releases; Products owns edition size, numbering, and remaining availability while the canonical creative record stays in Art, Work, or Research.
- **Subscriptions** — billing cadence, renewal behavior, cancellation method; recurring commerce requires *more* clarity than a one-time purchase, not less.
- **Commissions** — process and stages, expected client inputs, deposits, ownership and licensing principles; evaluated through enquiry and proposal, never purchased casually.

**Hybrid offers** (a service with a digital deliverable, a subscription with advisory sessions) keep one primary family based on the main value exchanged. Secondary components are described as included elements, not a second taxonomy.

---

## 8. Product Hierarchy

```text
Level 0 — Products Universe
Level 1 — Catalogue (permanent) and Curated Displays (may change)
Level 2 — Product Families (digital, physical, service, license, edition, subscription, commission)
Level 3 — Collections (curated groupings; do not own the canonical record)
Level 4 — Product Record (the canonical source of commercial truth)
Level 5 — Variants, Plans, Editions, or Service Options
```

Attributes support comparison; tags support editorial discovery. Neither becomes an uncontrolled public label cloud.

## 9. Canonical Records and Cross-Universe Relationships

**Origin truth stays separate from commercial truth.** Work, Research, Lab, and Art own their respective canonical records; Products owns the offer, price state, availability, and support record.

Origin records may show a restrained commercial link (`available as an edition`, `license this system`); product records reciprocally show origin district and a deliberate route back. Cross-universe links use relational language, never generic labels: `developed from this research`, `edition of this artwork`.

**Commercial status never alters canonical meaning.** An artwork remains Art after sale. A project remains Work after its associated product is discontinued. Research remains accessible as research even when a paid publication derives from it.

---

## 10. Homepage and Page System

**Homepage sequence:** Threshold (identity, positioning, routes to catalogue and current display) → Current Display (a spacious showroom zone, convincing with one item, controlled with several) → Catalogue Plane (the permanent structure as index) → Offer Types (spatial thresholds, not retail categories) → Practice Connections (a small number of meaningful relationships, never an automated feed) → Store Information (delivery, licensing, returns, support — accessible without starting checkout).

**Reusable page types**, each with one job: Products Homepage (curated entry) · Catalogue Index (systematic, searchable) · Product Family Page (structural, not campaign-driven) · Collection Page (narrative, temporary or permanent) · **Product Detail Page** (identity → value proposition → price → availability → primary action → inclusions/exclusions → variants → specifications → fulfilment → limitations → origin cross-link → support — essential commercial information always before extended narrative) · Comparison View (stable dimensions, plain language, no manufactured differences) · Cart/Selection (never a promotional surface) · Checkout (focused and linear; identity and safe-exit routes stay available) · Confirmation Page · Product Archive (documentary, not promotional) · Store Information Pages.

---

## 11. From Interest to Purchase

Nine stages, each a checkpoint rather than a funnel: **Interest** → **Orientation** (what kind of offer is this, is it active) → **Evaluation** (proposition, price basis, rights, provenance) → **Selection** (consequences visible before commitment) → **Commitment** (the action label names the actual commitment — purchase, reserve, apply, book) → **Review** (final verification before submission) → **Confirmation** (direct language, durable reference) → **Fulfilment** → **Support and Continuity**.

---

## 12. Pricing and Commercial Information

Price appears early enough to support evaluation, never hidden behind unnecessary interaction. Where a fixed price doesn't apply, the actual basis is stated: *from a stated amount*, *quotation required*, *hourly or package basis*. The interface distinguishes an indicative price from a binding one.

**Prohibited:** misleading crossed-out prices, hidden recurring charges, ambiguous free trials, fabricated anchoring tiers. Any discount must have a real basis and a stated duration.

---

## 13. Availability and Lifecycle States

Every product record has an explicit state, describing real operations rather than implied through styling:

**Forthcoming** (announced, not yet committable) · **Available** · **Limited** (a stated bounded condition) · **Made to Order** · **Preorder** (commitment before fulfilment; risks and terms explicit) · **Temporarily Unavailable** (no promised date unless credible) · **Capacity Closed** · **Sold Out** (visible for documentary purposes) · **Discontinued** (may identify a successor) · **Superseded** (previous record stays available for compatibility) · **Archived** · **Regionally Unavailable** · **Free Access** (access or attribution conditions may still apply).

---

## 14. Fulfilment, Licensing, and Support

Every active offer states how value reaches the customer — shipping, download, account access, scheduled session, license issuance — with timing and dependencies clear. License information layers as a plain-language summary, key permissions, and a route to full legal terms — the summary must never contradict the governing license. Support is proportional and explicit *before* acquisition; Products must never imply indefinite personal support where none exists. Existing customers can locate documentation and order references without reconstructing their original purchase journey.

---

## 15. Product Presentation

The **summary unit** contains only what's needed to judge whether deeper evaluation is worthwhile — never a miniature product page. Media is selected by informational purpose, not quantity. Specifications form an ordered layer, comparable to a material schedule — only applicable fields appear. Limitations and exclusions live inside the evaluation flow, not hidden in policy pages. Related offers are used sparingly, based on real relationships, never producing an endless recommendation loop.

---

## 16. Navigation, Search, and Discovery

Navigation operates at three distinguishable levels — global (Products is one district in the shared system; no unrelated e-commerce header), local (Overview, Catalogue, Families, Collections, Archive, Store Information), and contextual (product to family, product to canonical origin, archived offer to successor — each relationship named clearly) — so a visitor always knows whether they're in a curated display, family, catalogue, or archive, and when a link will leave Products.

**Search and filtering scale to the catalogue's actual size** — a small catalogue never imitates a large marketplace, even though the underlying content model should support future growth from the beginning. Useful filter dimensions (product family, availability, format, collection, price range) are exposed only when they provide real value. Filters show their effect clearly, remain removable individually, provide a full reset, and preserve browsing position when returning from a product. Search considers product names, descriptions, families, collections, and origin relationships; archived and unavailable offers may appear when relevant, but their status must be unmistakable.

---

## 17. Architectural Language, Responsive Behavior, and Accessibility

The district expresses threshold, display, and cataloguing through spatial logic — never theatrical shop imitation. It should not become a product-card wall as its dominant identity, an endless promotional homepage, a marketplace clone, or a dark-pattern conversion funnel.

Empty space gives an offer presence and separates commercial families, distinguishes curation from catalogue density, and creates pauses before commitment — but must never create excessive scrolling or conceal comparison information. Motion may resemble revealing a display bay, sliding a specimen table, or moving from overview to detail; it must never delay information, create false urgency, or interfere with checkout and support.

**Responsive behavior preserves information hierarchy across devices** rather than shrinking desktop composition. On small screens: a deliberate linear sequence, price and availability easy to locate, variants and filters touch-accessible, no hover-dependent disclosure. Accessibility requirements are non-negotiable: semantic hierarchy, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, descriptive media alternatives, sufficient target sizes, reduced motion, and accessible cart, checkout, enquiry, and cancellation paths.

> Immersion is optional. Access is not.

---

## 18. Empty, Early, and Transitional States

**Before launch:** a controlled threshold stating that offers are being prepared — never invented inventory, false release dates, or empty product placeholders. **Small catalogue:** generous presentation without artificial categories, filters, or collection structures. **Forthcoming catalogue:** forthcoming offers stay distinguishable from active ones, stating clearly what's confirmed versus provisional. **No matching results:** the interface shows the active conditions, why no entries appear, and a route back to the complete catalogue. **Unavailable offer:** the record stays visible when it has documentary, support, or provenance value, with the reason for unavailability stated clearly. **Failed transaction or request:** failure states identify the affected stage, preserve valid information when safe, distinguish payment/access/scheduling/technical problems, and provide a direct recovery or support path.

---

## 19. Governance, Scaling, and Cross-Domain Dependencies

This document owns qualification of offers as products, district philosophy, product families, homepage structure, the transaction journey, and lifecycle vocabulary. It does not own global navigation, payment infrastructure, tax treatment, or technical implementation.

The architecture supports growth toward a future studio or company through canonical records, durable families, and explicit lifecycle states — growth adds depth, not a foundational redesign. Future records may distinguish creator, studio, collaborator, and rights holder without hard-coding the current personal identity in a way that blocks organizational evolution. **Commercial retirement must not erase design history** — the archive preserves origin relationship, specifications, and prior availability state even after a product is discontinued.

**Cross-domain dependencies — corrected from v1.0 §24.** v1.0 assigned seller identity, rights-holder information, commercial disclosures, support contacts, policies, and legal terms to an undifferentiated "About, Legal, and Business Structure" heading. That assignment conflicted with `ABOUT_UNIVERSE.md` §27, which explicitly excludes "transaction information" from what About owns. This v1.1 draft applies the approved three-way ownership split instead:

- **The global legal and administrative utility layer** — the "Legal and administrative information" utility named in `INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5, alongside Search and Index, and governed outside any single district — owns seller legal identity, rights-holder identity, privacy policy, general terms, statutory disclosures, and company or individual business identity. This is platform-wide legal and administrative information, not specific to any one offer.
- **Products** (this document) owns offer-specific pricing, availability, fulfilment, licensing summaries, support, offer-specific commercial conditions, and the transaction flow — everything that varies by offer, as detailed throughout this document.
- **About** owns personal and professional identity, authorship, biography, trajectory, practice structure, and organizational evolution. About does not own transaction information or platform-wide legal disclosures.

This resolves the ownership question without inventing legal wording, seller details, business-registration information, tax rules, or jurisdictional policy text — none of which this document, or the platform's Information Architecture, has approved content for yet. It states *who owns the content*, not what the content says.

## 20. Approved Foundational Decisions

Reproduced verbatim from `PRODUCTS_UNIVERSE_v1.0.md` §25, in full, for traceability. No item added, removed, or reworded.

1. **Products operationalizes value.**
2. Products is the platform’s commercial district, not a detached shop and not the governing identity of the platform.
3. A product is a resolved and responsibly offerable form of value with defined deliverables, conditions, provision, and support.
4. Work, Research, Lab, and Art retain canonical ownership of their respective records; Products owns the commercial offer and transaction layer.
5. An artwork remains canonical in Art even when it becomes available as an original, edition, license, or commission.
6. An experiment becomes offerable only when a defined version crosses a threshold of value, stability, provision, commercial clarity, support, and lifecycle responsibility.
7. The district supports digital products, physical objects, services, licenses, editions, subscriptions, commissions, and hybrid offers.
8. Its spatial concept is **The Quiet Exchange Hall**, combining showroom, catalogue archive, evaluation table, and counter.
9. The homepage remains curated and atmospheric; the catalogue carries comprehensive discovery functions.
10. Product pages present essential commercial information before extended narrative context.
11. Pricing, availability, fulfilment, licensing, recurring conditions, and support are primary information layers.
12. The visitor journey proceeds from interest to orientation, evaluation, selection, commitment, review, confirmation, fulfilment, and support.
13. Commerce must not use false urgency, hidden costs, manipulative defaults, obstructive cancellation, or misleading scarcity.
14. Products uses explicit lifecycle states for forthcoming, available, limited, made-to-order, preorder, temporarily unavailable, capacity closed, sold out, discontinued, superseded, archived, regionally unavailable, and free-access offers.
15. Inactive products remain accessible when they have documentary, provenance, compatibility, or support value.
16. Architectural identity is expressed through hierarchy, rhythm, spatial silence, cataloguing, and measured transitions—not through a generic e-commerce template or literal shop simulation.
17. The architecture must scale from a small personal catalogue to a multi-author studio or company without foundational redesign.
18. Commercial retirement must not erase the practice’s creative, professional, experimental, or institutional history.

---

## 21. Final Definition

Products defines what qualifies as a product, when experimental work becomes offerable, and how commerce stays subordinate to the wider platform identity while remaining exact and dependable on its own ground. It preserves canonical ownership in Work, Research, Lab, and Art while owning the commercial offer outright.

**Products is where value becomes offerable.**
