## 1. Purpose

The Products Universe is the commercial district of the Personal Platform.

It is where resolved outcomes become offerable: purchasable, licensable, reservable, commissionable, subscribable, downloadable, or otherwise exchangeable under explicit terms.

Products does not define the identity of the overall platform. It provides the infrastructure through which selected parts of the practice can enter into exchange with others.

Its governing principle is:

> **Products operationalizes value.**

Work demonstrates capability.  
Research develops understanding.  
Lab tests and transforms.  
Art concentrates perception.  
Products converts a sufficiently resolved outcome into a clear offer.

The district must function simultaneously as:

- a curated showroom
- a legible catalogue
- a trustworthy transaction environment
- a service and licensing interface
- a record of releases and editions
- a support threshold after acquisition
- a scalable commercial foundation for a future studio or company

It must remain coherent when the catalogue contains only a few offers and remain navigable if commercial activity expands substantially.

## 2. The Role of Commerce Within the Platform

Commerce is a capability of the platform, not its governing identity.

The wider Personal Platform exists to represent one coherent design practice across architecture, computation, software, artificial intelligence, research, experimentation, art, and future forms of production. Products allows certain outcomes of that practice to be exchanged without forcing every district to behave like a store.

The commercial layer must therefore remain:

- visible without becoming dominant
- accessible without interrupting contemplation or inquiry
- direct without becoming aggressive
- integrated without flattening every form of work into merchandise
- scalable without converting the platform into a generic marketplace

Products may support revenue, professional growth, distribution, collaboration, and future company operations. It must not retroactively redefine the purpose of Work, Research, Lab, or Art.

A visitor should be able to experience the entire platform without being treated primarily as a customer. When the visitor enters Products, however, the commercial conditions must become explicit and dependable.

## 3. What Qualifies as a Product?

A product is not defined only by physical form or by the presence of a price.

Within this platform, a **product** is a resolved offer with:

1. a defined recipient or user
2. a comprehensible value proposition
3. an identifiable deliverable, access right, or service outcome
4. explicit commercial or access conditions
5. sufficient stability to be represented truthfully
6. a repeatable or intentionally bounded method of provision
7. a support, fulfilment, or completion path
8. an accountable owner

A product may be free. It may be physical, digital, temporal, recurring, bespoke, licensed, limited, or service-based. What makes it a product is not simply that it can be displayed, but that it can be responsibly offered.

### 3.1 Product Rather Than Work

Work is the canonical record of professional capability, process, collaboration, and resolved project outcomes.

Products owns the commercial offer derived from or associated with that work.

A professional project remains in Work even when it produces something sellable. Products may offer a tool, publication, template, consultation, license, component, or service connected to that project, but it does not replace the Work case study.

The distinction is:

- **Work asks:** What was accomplished, under what conditions, and what capability does it demonstrate?
- **Products asks:** What can another person obtain, under what terms, at what cost, and what happens after acquisition?

### 3.2 Product Rather Than Art

Art remains the canonical home of the artwork.

The Art record protects the work’s artistic identity, perceptual experience, series relationships, medium, period, context, and interpretation. Products owns only the transaction layer attached to an artwork when it becomes available as an original, edition, print, object, digital release, license, commission, or other offer.

The distinction is:

- **Art asks:** What is the work, and how should it be encountered?
- **Products asks:** In what form, if any, can it be acquired, commissioned, licensed, or reserved?

The commercial state must never overwrite the canonical Art record. An artwork remains visible and meaningful in Art after it is sold, unavailable, discontinued, or no longer offered.

### 3.3 Product Rather Than Lab

Lab is the canonical environment for unstable experiments, prototypes, versions, tests, failures, and transformations.

Products may offer something originating in Lab only when the offer itself is sufficiently stable and responsibly bounded. The underlying investigation may remain open, but the offered version must have clear capabilities, limitations, requirements, and support expectations.

The distinction is:

- **Lab asks:** What is being tested, changed, or learned?
- **Products asks:** What stable value can be delivered now, and under what conditions?

An experimental origin is not a defect. Ambiguous operational status is.

### 3.4 Product Rather Than Research

Research remains the canonical record of questions, methods, evidence, arguments, and knowledge development.

Products may operationalize research through a publication, dataset, framework, toolkit, course, license, advisory service, or other structured offer. It must not reduce the research record to sales copy or imply certainty beyond what the research supports.

The distinction is:

- **Research asks:** What is being investigated or understood?
- **Products asks:** What usable form of that knowledge can be accessed or acquired?

### 3.5 Offers That Do Not Yet Qualify

An outcome should not enter Products when:

- its purpose cannot be stated clearly
- its deliverable is undefined
- its availability is speculative
- its limitations are materially unknown
- its creator cannot support or fulfil it as represented
- pricing or access conditions are misleading or incomplete
- it depends on an unstable prototype without adequate disclosure
- it exists only as an idea with no credible offer pathway
- it would be safer or more truthful to present it as Lab, Research, Work, or Art

Products must never be used to simulate commercial readiness.

## 4. When Does an Experiment Become Offerable?

An experiment becomes offerable when a defined version crosses a threshold of responsibility.

It does not need to become final in an absolute sense. Digital tools, services, methods, and systems may continue evolving. The offered state must nevertheless be stable enough that another person can understand what they are receiving and reasonably rely on the stated conditions.

### 4.1 Offerability Test

Before an experimental outcome enters Products, it should satisfy the following questions:

#### Value

- Is the intended use or benefit understandable?
- Is the intended user or client identifiable?
- Does the offer solve, enable, provide, or meaningfully deliver something?

#### Definition

- Is the offered version clearly bounded?
- Are included and excluded elements stated?
- Can the deliverable, access right, or service outcome be described without relying on speculative language?

#### Reliability

- Does the offer perform consistently enough for its stated purpose?
- Are known limitations disclosed?
- Are dependencies, compatibility conditions, or required skills visible?

#### Provision

- Can it be delivered, accessed, scheduled, licensed, produced, or completed as represented?
- Is there an owner responsible for fulfilment?
- Are lead times or capacity constraints understood?

#### Commercial clarity

- Are price, payment basis, license, duration, renewal, cancellation, edition, or commission conditions explicit where applicable?
- Does the visitor know what action constitutes a commitment?

#### Support

- Is there a defined route for questions, defects, access problems, updates, or post-purchase needs?
- Is the support boundary truthful and sustainable?

#### Lifecycle

- Can the offer be updated, paused, retired, archived, or superseded without losing its history or confusing prior customers?

If these conditions are not met, the outcome remains in Lab or another canonical district.

### 4.2 Experimental Products

A product may still be experimental when this is part of the offer, provided that:

- the experimental status is explicit
- the tested version is identifiable
- limitations and instability are disclosed
- access conditions reflect the level of maturity
- support expectations are bounded
- the visitor is not led to expect production-grade certainty

Terms such as beta, preview, pilot, early access, prototype edition, or experimental license must correspond to genuine operational distinctions. They are not promotional labels.

### 4.3 Maturation Path

A typical path may be:

**Research question → Lab experiment → validated version → defined offer → Product release**

Other paths are equally valid:

**Art record → edition decision → Product offer**  
**Work method → repeatable service → Product offer**  
**Research publication → licensed or purchasable format → Product offer**

The movement into Products creates a new commercial record. It does not relocate or erase the canonical origin.

## 5. Core Spatial Concept

### The Quiet Exchange Hall

The Products Universe is conceived as a quiet exchange hall within the architectural metaverse.

It combines four spatial conditions:

1. **The showroom** — selected offers receive space, scale, and presence.
2. **The catalogue archive** — the full body of offers is ordered, searchable, and durable.
3. **The evaluation table** — specifications, comparisons, terms, and provenance are examined clearly.
4. **The counter** — commitment, payment, reservation, application, or contact happens directly and without ambiguity.

The district should feel composed rather than crowded, exact rather than promotional, and calm without becoming passive.

Its architectural references may include:

- measured display bays
- modular shelving and storage systems
- specimen tables
- cataloguing marks
- edition and serial notation
- plans, elevations, and sections
- material schedules
- controlled thresholds
- service counters
- loading, transfer, and dispatch logic

These references should guide hierarchy, rhythm, framing, and movement. The interface must not become a literal simulation of a shop interior.

## 6. Storefront Philosophy

### 6.1 Curation Before Volume

Products should feel selected, not accumulated.

The homepage must not attempt to expose the entire catalogue. It should establish the district, present a controlled current display, and provide clear routes into the permanent catalogue.

The system must work with one offer, several offers, or a large catalogue without artificial filler.

### 6.2 Understanding Before Commitment

Every offer must allow the visitor to answer:

- What is it?
- Who is it for?
- What value does it provide?
- What exactly is included?
- What is excluded?
- How is it delivered, accessed, scheduled, licensed, or fulfilled?
- What does it cost?
- What constraints apply?
- What support is provided?
- What happens after the next action?

No atmospheric treatment may obscure these answers.

### 6.3 Commerce Without Pressure

Products rejects manipulative retail patterns.

It should not use:

- false scarcity
- invented countdowns
- hidden fees
- automatically selected extras
- misleading comparison prices
- obstructive cancellation
- confusing subscription consent
- visually dominant discount language
- aggressive cross-selling
- attention traps that interfere with evaluation

Real scarcity, capacity, production windows, edition limits, lead times, or release deadlines must be expressed plainly and accurately.

### 6.4 Trust as Architecture

Trust must be built into the information structure rather than added as reassurance near checkout.

The visitor should be able to locate, when relevant:

- price and currency
- whether taxes are included or calculated later
- physical or digital delivery method
- production or fulfilment timing
- license scope
- subscription cadence and renewal behavior
- commission process
- availability or edition status
- cancellation, return, refund, or withdrawal conditions
- seller, studio, author, or rights-holder identity
- privacy and payment-security information
- contact and support routes

This information is part of the designed offer, not legal residue.

### 6.5 Context Without Duplication

Products should expose provenance while respecting canonical ownership.

A product record may show concise origin context and link to:

- a Work project
- an Art record or series
- a Research investigation
- a Lab experiment or version history

Products must not duplicate the full narrative, gallery experience, research argument, or experimental record. It should explain the commercial relationship and allow the visitor to cross the threshold deliberately.

### 6.6 Exchange, Not Merchandise

The district may contain practical or commercial objects, but it should not present the practice as a merchandise brand.

The central question is not, “What can be sold?” It is:

> “Which outcomes can be offered responsibly, and in what form does their value become transferable?”

## 7. Product Families and Commercial Forms

The hierarchy should organize offers by the nature of what is exchanged, not by temporary marketing themes.

The following families are approved as structural capabilities. They do not imply that any actual product currently exists.

### 7.1 Digital Products

Digital products provide files, software, tools, data, templates, publications, media, or access to a defined digital resource.

Their records may require:

- file or platform format
- version
- system requirements
- compatibility
- access or download method
- update policy
- license scope
- documentation
- support boundary

### 7.2 Physical Objects

Physical objects include designed, fabricated, printed, assembled, or produced goods.

Their records may require:

- dimensions
- materials
- weight
- fabrication method
- finish or variation
- stock or production state
- shipping region
- production lead time
- care information
- return conditions

### 7.3 Services

Services exchange time, expertise, analysis, facilitation, design work, consultation, teaching, review, or another defined professional activity.

A service must not be presented like a stock item. Its record should clarify:

- purpose and intended client
- scope
- deliverables
- process or stages
- duration or effort basis
- availability or capacity
- prerequisites
- price basis or quotation method
- exclusions
- next commitment step

### 7.4 Licenses

Licenses grant defined rights to use software, data, media, methods, intellectual property, designs, or other protected material.

The offer must distinguish:

- what is licensed
- who may use it
- permitted uses
- prohibited uses
- territory, term, or seat limits
- modification and redistribution rights
- attribution requirements
- update or support entitlements
- commercial versus personal use where applicable

The legal wording belongs to future legal documentation, but the product experience must make the practical meaning legible.

### 7.5 Editions

Editions are intentionally bounded releases of an artwork, object, publication, digital work, or other reproducible outcome.

The canonical creative record remains in Art, Work, Research, or another origin district. Products owns:

- the available edition form
- edition size
- numbering or authentication method
- format and material
- price
- remaining availability when appropriate
- fulfilment conditions
- acquisition history or sold-out state where appropriate

Edition scarcity must be factual and durable.

### 7.6 Subscriptions and Recurring Access

Subscriptions provide ongoing access, delivery, service, updates, membership, or another recurring benefit.

Their records must make visible:

- what recurs
- billing cadence
- renewal behavior
- included access or deliverables
- usage limits
- minimum term, if any
- cancellation method and effective date
- price changes or notification principles
- consequences of ending access

Recurring commerce requires greater clarity than a one-time purchase, not less.

### 7.7 Commissions

Commissions provide a structured path for requesting bespoke work.

A commission record should explain:

- what may be commissioned
- degree of customization
- eligibility or fit
- process and stages
- expected client inputs
- indicative timing
- capacity or application state
- pricing basis or quotation process
- deposits and commitment thresholds
- review or revision boundaries
- ownership and licensing principles

A commission is not purchased casually. The interface should support evaluation, enquiry, qualification, proposal, and agreement as distinct stages.

### 7.8 Hybrid Offers

Some offers combine families—for example, a service with a digital deliverable, a physical object with a digital license, or a subscription with advisory sessions.

Each hybrid offer should still have one primary family based on the main value exchanged. Secondary components should be described as included elements rather than creating an uncontrolled taxonomy.

## 8. Product Hierarchy

The hierarchy separates permanent records from temporary curation.

### Level 0 — Products Universe

The commercial district as a whole.

### Level 1 — Catalogue and Curated Displays

Two parallel systems:

- **Catalogue:** the complete set of current and historically relevant product records.
- **Curated displays:** selected releases, collections, themes, or editorial groupings.

The catalogue is permanent. Curated displays may change.

### Level 2 — Product Families

Durable classifications based on the principal form of exchange:

- digital product
- physical object
- service
- license
- edition
- subscription
- commission

Future families may be added only when a genuinely different commercial form cannot be represented by the existing structure.

### Level 3 — Collections

Collections are curated groupings based on origin, concept, release, audience, period, use, or theme.

A product may belong to multiple collections. Collections do not own the canonical product record.

### Level 4 — Product Record

The canonical source of commercial truth for an offer.

It contains the structured information needed to:

- understand the offer
- evaluate fit
- transact or enquire
- fulfil or access
- support the customer or client
- manage lifecycle status
- connect to canonical origin records
- preserve the offer after retirement

### Level 5 — Variants, Plans, Editions, or Service Options

These represent purchasable or selectable forms of the same core offer.

They should be used only when the options share the same fundamental identity. Materially different offers require separate product records.

### Supporting Classification — Attributes and Tags

Attributes support comparison and filtering. Tags support editorial discovery.

Neither should become an uncontrolled public label cloud.

## 9. Canonical Records and Cross-Universe Relationships

Products must preserve a clear separation between **origin truth** and **commercial truth**.

### 9.1 Canonical Ownership

- Work owns the canonical professional project or capability record.
- Research owns the canonical investigation or knowledge record.
- Lab owns the canonical experiment, test, branch, and iteration record.
- Art owns the canonical artwork, series, collection, medium, and period record.
- Products owns the canonical offer, price state, availability, transaction terms, fulfilment, license, and support record.

### 9.2 Bidirectional Linking

Origin records may show a restrained commercial link when an associated offer exists, such as:

- available as an edition
- acquire this work
- access the toolkit
- license this system
- commission a related service

Product records should reciprocally show:

- origin district
- canonical source title
- nature of the relationship
- deliberate route to the full origin record

### 9.3 No Silent Redirection

A visitor should never be moved between districts without understanding why.

Cross-universe links should use relational language, not generic labels. Examples of relationship types include:

- developed from this research
- emerged from this experiment
- associated with this professional project
- edition of this artwork
- service based on this method

### 9.4 Commercial Status Does Not Alter Canonical Meaning

Selling, licensing, archiving, discontinuing, or withdrawing an offer must not remove or rewrite the origin record.

An artwork remains Art after sale.  
A project remains Work after its associated product is discontinued.  
An experiment remains documented in Lab after a stable version is released.  
Research remains accessible as research even when a paid publication or service derives from it.

## 10. Products Universe Homepage

The homepage is the threshold to the commercial district, not a compressed marketplace.

Its purpose is to:

- establish the identity of Products
- communicate the governing principle of responsible exchange
- present a controlled current display
- reveal the permanent catalogue structure
- support both exploration and directed acquisition
- connect commerce to the wider practice
- expose practical store information

### 10.1 Threshold

A restrained opening field introduces Products as a district.

It should contain:

- district name
- concise positioning statement
- primary route to the catalogue
- secondary route to the current display

The threshold should use architectural framing, proportion, depth, or linework rather than a conventional promotional hero banner.

### 10.2 Current Display

A limited selection of current offers occupies a spacious showroom zone.

This area may represent:

- a release
- a selected edition
- a service opening
- a collection
- a publication
- a digital tool
- a forthcoming offer

The module must remain convincing when it contains one item and controlled when it contains several.

### 10.3 Catalogue Plane

The permanent commercial structure becomes visible as an index or navigational drawing.

It should provide direct access to:

- all products
- product families
- collections
- current availability states when useful
- archive

### 10.4 Offer Types

The homepage may introduce the major commercial forms as spatial thresholds rather than generic retail categories.

Their purpose is orientation, not visual repetition.

### 10.5 Practice Connections

A restrained contextual zone explains that offers originate in the wider practice.

It should show a small number of meaningful relationships to Work, Research, Lab, or Art, not an automated feed of everything commercially connected.

### 10.6 Store Information

The closing zone gives calm access to:

- delivery and digital access
- service process
- licensing
- commissions
- subscriptions
- returns or refunds
- support
- seller or studio information
- commercial policies

A visitor should not need to begin checkout to understand the conditions of exchange.

## 11. Page and Layout System

The district should use a limited family of reusable page types. Offers populate the system; they do not each invent a new storefront.

### 11.1 Products Homepage

**Purpose:** Curated entry and orientation.  
**Character:** Spacious, atmospheric, selective, and architectural.

### 11.2 Catalogue Index

**Purpose:** Present the complete current catalogue.  
**Character:** Systematic, denser, searchable, and highly navigable.

It should support, when catalogue scale justifies them:

- search
- sorting
- filtering
- active-filter visibility
- understandable result counts
- empty-state recovery
- persistent browsing state
- clear distinction between available and archival records

### 11.3 Product Family Page

**Purpose:** Explain and group a durable commercial form.  
**Character:** Structural rather than campaign-driven.

Each family page should provide:

- concise family definition
- relevant evaluation criteria
- current offers
- applicable status distinctions
- route to related store information

### 11.4 Collection or Release Page

**Purpose:** Present a curated grouping united by concept, origin, period, audience, or release logic.  
**Character:** More narrative than a family page, less extensive than a Work or Art record.

Collections may be temporary or permanent. Removing a collection from featured navigation must not remove its products from the catalogue.

### 11.5 Product Detail Page

**Purpose:** Move the visitor from interest to informed evaluation and then to an appropriate commitment.

The default information sequence is:

1. product identity
2. product family and essential descriptor
3. primary media or representation
4. concise value proposition
5. price or price basis
6. availability state
7. primary action
8. included deliverables, access, or service scope
9. variants, plans, editions, or options
10. specifications and compatibility
11. fulfilment, delivery, scheduling, or access process
12. licensing, subscription, or commission conditions
13. limitations and exclusions
14. origin and canonical cross-link
15. support and policy routes
16. related offers, used sparingly

Essential commercial information must appear before extended narrative context.

### 11.6 Evaluation or Comparison View

**Purpose:** Help visitors compare genuinely comparable offers, plans, licenses, or service scopes.

Comparison should use stable dimensions and plain language. It must not manufacture artificial differences or push a predetermined option through visual manipulation.

### 11.7 Cart or Selection Space

**Purpose:** Review selected one-time acquisitions before checkout.

It should show:

- selected offers
- variant, edition, plan, or license
- quantity where relevant
- individual and combined cost
- estimated additional charges when known
- delivery or access distinctions
- editing and removal controls
- next action

The cart should not become a promotional surface.

Services, commissions, subscriptions, or licenses may use different selection and commitment paths when a conventional cart would misrepresent the transaction.

### 11.8 Checkout or Commitment Flow

**Purpose:** Complete payment, reservation, subscription, application, enquiry, or another explicit commitment with minimum uncertainty.

The flow should be focused and linear. Global exploration may recede, while identity, support, policy, and safe-exit routes remain available.

The exact legal and technical checkout structure is outside this document.

### 11.9 Confirmation and Fulfilment Page

**Purpose:** Confirm what occurred and explain what happens next.

It should identify:

- transaction or request state
- acquired or requested offer
- payment or enquiry status
- delivery, access, scheduling, or review steps
- expected timing
- reference information
- support route

### 11.10 Product Archive

**Purpose:** Preserve inactive, retired, sold-out, superseded, or historically relevant offers.  
**Character:** Documentary rather than promotional.

Archived pages remain linkable and understandable but cannot be mistaken for active offers.

### 11.11 Store Information Pages

**Purpose:** Centralize stable guidance for delivery, digital access, licensing, subscriptions, commissions, service processes, returns, support, privacy, and terms.

The exact legal text remains outside this document.

## 12. From Interest to Purchase

The Products Universe supports a deliberate journey rather than immediate conversion pressure.

### Stage 1 — Interest

The visitor encounters an offer through:

- the Products homepage
- the catalogue
- a collection
- search
- a direct link
- a related Work, Research, Lab, or Art record

The first encounter should communicate identity, relevance, broad format, and status.

### Stage 2 — Orientation

The visitor understands:

- where they are
- what kind of offer this is
- whether it is active
- whether it is one-time, recurring, limited, bespoke, or licensed
- the next route for deeper evaluation

### Stage 3 — Evaluation

The visitor examines:

- proposition
- contents or scope
- media
- specifications
- compatibility or prerequisites
- price or quotation basis
- availability
- delivery or service process
- rights and restrictions
- limitations
- provenance
- support

### Stage 4 — Selection

Where applicable, the visitor chooses:

- format
- variant
- edition
- quantity
- license
- plan
- term
- service scope
- commission pathway

The consequences of each choice must be visible before commitment.

### Stage 5 — Commitment

The primary action must describe the actual commitment, such as:

- purchase
- reserve
- subscribe
- request a quote
- apply for a commission
- book a consultation
- obtain a license
- join a release notification

Generic or misleading action labels should be avoided.

### Stage 6 — Review

Before final commitment, the visitor verifies:

- selected offer and form
- cost and currency
- recurring conditions
- taxes or additional charges when known
- delivery or access method
- schedule or lead time
- license or service conditions

### Stage 7 — Confirmation

The platform confirms what occurred in direct language and provides a durable reference.

### Stage 8 — Fulfilment or Access

The visitor receives the physical delivery, digital access, service scheduling, license, subscription entitlement, commission process, or other promised outcome.

### Stage 9 — Support and Continuity

The visitor can obtain help, documentation, updates, renewal information, or post-purchase guidance without reconstructing the original journey.

## 13. Pricing and Commercial Information

Pricing should be treated as a primary information layer.

### 13.1 Price Visibility

For directly purchasable offers, price should appear early enough to support evaluation and should not be hidden behind unnecessary interaction.

Where a fixed price is not appropriate, the page should state the actual basis, such as:

- from a stated amount
- priced by scope
- quotation required
- application followed by proposal
- hourly, daily, package, seat, usage, edition, or subscription basis

The interface must distinguish an indicative price from a binding price.

### 13.2 Currency and Taxes

The displayed currency must be explicit. Tax treatment, regional differences, and additional charges should be shown as early and accurately as the future legal and technical system allows.

### 13.3 Price Integrity

Products should not use:

- misleading crossed-out prices
- permanent pseudo-discounts
- hidden recurring charges
- ambiguous free trials
- unannounced renewal
- fabricated anchoring tiers

Any discount, introductory period, or temporary price must have a real basis and a clear duration.

### 13.4 Services and Commissions

For services and commissions, the price presentation should support informed enquiry without implying that a bespoke engagement is fully defined before scoping.

The page may communicate:

- fixed package price
- starting price
- day or hour rate
- typical range
- quotation process
- deposit basis

Only truthful and operationally sustainable structures should be used.

## 14. Availability and Lifecycle States

Every product record must have an explicit lifecycle state.

The approved conceptual vocabulary is:

### 14.1 Forthcoming

The offer has been announced but is not yet available for commitment.

The page should state what is known, what remains provisional, and whether notification registration is available. It must not simulate active stock or accept payment unless operating as a clearly defined preorder.

### 14.2 Available

The offer can currently be acquired, licensed, booked, subscribed to, or requested under the stated conditions.

### 14.3 Limited

The offer is available under a real bounded condition such as edition size, production quantity, capacity, time window, or regional access.

The nature of the limit must be stated.

### 14.4 Made to Order

The offer is produced or configured after commitment. Lead time, process, variability, and cancellation conditions must be visible.

### 14.5 Preorder

Commitment occurs before fulfilment or final release. Expected timing, risks, payment conditions, and cancellation terms must be explicit.

### 14.6 Temporarily Unavailable

The offer is expected to return, but cannot currently be acquired. The page should avoid promising a date unless one is operationally credible.

### 14.7 Capacity Closed

A service, commission, or subscription intake is temporarily closed because capacity has been reached. The offer remains active in principle but cannot accept new commitments.

### 14.8 Sold Out

A finite stock or edition has been fully allocated. The record remains visible for documentary purposes and may link to related active offers without implying that the sold-out item will return.

### 14.9 Discontinued

The offer is no longer provided and is not expected to return in the same form.

A discontinued record may identify a successor when a meaningful relationship exists.

### 14.10 Superseded

A newer version or offer has replaced the active role of the current record. The previous record remains available for historical, licensing, compatibility, or support reasons.

### 14.11 Archived

The record is retained as part of the practice’s commercial history but is no longer active.

### 14.12 Regionally Unavailable

The offer exists but cannot be acquired in the visitor’s region. The reason and available alternatives should be stated when possible.

### 14.13 Free Access

No payment is required, but access, licensing, registration, attribution, or usage conditions may still apply.

Status language must reflect real operations. Availability must never be implied solely through visual styling.

## 15. Fulfilment, Licensing, and Support

### 15.1 Fulfilment

Every active offer should state how value reaches the customer or client.

Possible fulfilment paths include:

- physical shipping
- collection
- digital download
- account or platform access
- emailed delivery
- scheduled session
- staged service engagement
- license issuance
- subscription activation
- commission process

The path should clarify timing, dependencies, and the visitor’s responsibilities.

### 15.2 Licensing

License information should be layered:

1. a plain-language practical summary
2. key permissions and restrictions
3. route to the complete legal terms

The plain-language summary must not contradict the governing license.

### 15.3 Support

Support should be proportional to the offer and explicit before acquisition.

A record may define:

- setup support
- documentation
- response channel
- support hours or response expectations
- update entitlement
- defect handling
- compatibility boundary
- service follow-up
- commission communication process

Products must not imply indefinite personal support where none exists.

### 15.4 Existing Customers and Clients

Support routes should not be hidden beneath acquisition-oriented content. Existing customers should be able to locate documentation, downloads, license information, order references, updates, or contact channels directly.

## 16. Product Presentation System

### 16.1 Product Summary Unit

The reusable summary unit should contain only what is necessary to determine whether deeper evaluation is relevant:

- primary image or preview
- product name
- product family
- concise descriptor
- price or price basis
- availability state
- edition, term, or delivery marker when essential

It must not become a miniature product page.

### 16.2 Media Roles

Media should be selected by informational purpose rather than quantity.

Possible roles include:

- identity image
- scale image
- detail image
- use-context image
- interface preview
- included-content overview
- material or fabrication detail
- motion or interaction demonstration
- service-process diagram
- license or plan comparison

Media should support evaluation without turning the page into an advertising sequence.

### 16.3 Specifications as Architecture

Specifications should form an ordered layer comparable to a material schedule, drawing legend, or technical register.

Depending on the offer, fields may include:

- dimensions
- material
- format
- file type
- version
- compatibility
- language
- duration
- edition
- seat count
- territory
- access period
- delivery method
- service stages
- support level

Only applicable fields should appear.

### 16.4 Limitations and Exclusions

What an offer does not include can be as important as what it includes.

Limitations, exclusions, prerequisites, and incompatible uses should be located within the evaluation flow, not hidden in policy pages.

### 16.5 Related Offers

Related products should be used sparingly and based on meaningful relationships:

- required companion
- compatible extension
- alternate format
- later version
- service supporting the product
- product derived from the same origin

The storefront should not produce endless recommendation loops.

## 17. Navigation Architecture

Navigation operates at three levels.

### 17.1 Global Platform Navigation

Products remains one district in the shared global system.

The global navigation must preserve:

- access to Home
- access to all major districts
- recognition that the visitor remains inside one platform
- a clear distinction between platform exploration and commercial utilities

Products must not introduce an unrelated e-commerce header.

### 17.2 Local Products Navigation

The approved primary structure is:

- Products overview
- Catalogue
- Product families
- Collections
- Archive
- Store information

Search, cart, account, downloads, or support may become persistent utilities when operationally justified.

### 17.3 Contextual Navigation

Contextual links connect:

- product to family
- product to collection
- product to canonical origin
- origin record to product offer
- archived offer to successor
- current version to previous version
- purchased offer to support or documentation

The relationship should be named clearly.

### 17.4 Orientation

Visitors should always understand:

- that they are inside Products
- whether they are in a curated display, family, catalogue, or archive
- the active availability state
- the status of any selection or transaction
- how to return to the previous level
- when a link will enter another district

Immersion must not weaken orientation.

## 18. Search, Filtering, and Discovery

### 18.1 Scale-Appropriate Complexity

A small catalogue should not imitate a large marketplace.

Search and advanced filtering should appear only when they provide real value. The underlying content model should support future growth from the beginning.

### 18.2 Potential Filter Dimensions

Useful future dimensions may include:

- product family
- availability
- format
- collection
- origin district
- delivery method
- license type
- edition type
- service mode
- subscription cadence
- price range

Only dimensions with meaningful variation should be exposed.

### 18.3 Filter Behavior

Filters should:

- show their effect clearly
- remain visible after results update
- be removable individually
- provide a full reset
- preserve browsing position when returning from a product
- produce an understandable empty state
- support shareable states when technically feasible

### 18.4 Search Behavior

Search should consider product names, concise descriptions, families, collections, attributes, origin relationships, and historical records.

Archived and unavailable offers may appear when relevant, but their status must be unmistakable.

## 19. Architectural Language Without Generic E-Commerce

Products should retain the shared architectural-metaverse language through spatial logic, not theatrical imitation.

### 19.1 Architectural Qualities

The district may express:

- threshold and compression
- display and distance
- plan-like indexing
- section-like information depth
- bays and modules
- schedules and catalogues
- serial notation
- material hierarchy
- measured transitions
- stable reference points

### 19.2 What It Must Avoid

The district should not become:

- a conventional product-card wall as its dominant identity
- an endless promotional homepage
- a marketplace clone
- a literal three-dimensional shop simulation
- a campaign site dependent on constant visual reinvention
- a dark-pattern conversion funnel
- an atmospheric interface that hides transaction essentials

### 19.3 Spatial Silence

Empty space gives an offer presence and allows evaluation without competition.

Silence should be used to:

- separate commercial families
- distinguish curation from catalogue density
- establish hierarchy around primary media
- create pauses before commitment
- prevent policy, specification, and narrative layers from collapsing together

Silence must not create excessive scrolling or conceal relevant comparison information.

### 19.4 Motion

Motion may resemble:

- revealing a display bay
- sliding a specimen table
- unfolding a schedule
- moving from overview to detail
- confirming transfer or selection

It must not delay information, create false urgency, or interfere with checkout and support.

## 20. Responsive and Accessible Experience

The storefront must preserve information hierarchy across devices rather than shrink desktop composition.

### Wide Screens

- use space to establish product presence
- coordinate media, specifications, and commercial information
- allow essential actions to remain stable during deeper reading
- support comparison without overwhelming the field

### Medium Screens

- reduce simultaneous zones
- preserve the relationship between representation, essential facts, and action
- convert complex tables or indexes into clear grouped structures

### Small Screens

- use a deliberate linear sequence
- keep price, availability, and primary action easy to locate
- make variants, plans, filters, and cart editing touch-accessible
- avoid hover-dependent disclosure
- ensure media does not displace essential information excessively

### Accessibility Principles

The district must support:

- semantic hierarchy
- keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- descriptive media alternatives
- legible status and price information
- sufficient target sizes
- zoom and text resizing
- reduced motion
- understandable form validation
- accessible cart, checkout, enquiry, and cancellation paths

> Immersion is optional. Access is not.

## 21. Empty, Early, and Transitional States

### 21.1 Before Launch

Products may present a controlled threshold stating that offers are being prepared. It should not use invented inventory, false release dates, or empty product placeholders.

### 21.2 Small Catalogue

A small catalogue receives generous presentation without artificial categories, filters, or collection structures.

### 21.3 Forthcoming Catalogue

Forthcoming offers should be distinguishable from active offers and should state what is confirmed versus provisional.

### 21.4 No Matching Results

The interface should show:

- the active conditions
- why no entries appear
- how to remove or modify filters
- a route back to the complete catalogue

### 21.5 Unavailable Offer

The record remains visible when it has documentary, support, or provenance value. The reason for unavailability must be clear.

### 21.6 Failed Transaction or Request

Failure states should:

- identify the affected stage
- preserve valid information when safe
- distinguish payment, access, scheduling, stock, and technical problems
- provide a direct recovery or support path

## 22. Scaling Toward a Future Studio or Company

Products must remain valid if the personal platform evolves into a studio or company.

### 22.1 Catalogue Scale

The architecture supports growth through:

- canonical product records
- durable product families
- controlled collections
- structured attributes
- explicit lifecycle states
- reusable page types
- archive continuity

Growth should add depth, not require a foundational redesign.

### 22.2 Offer-Type Scale

The system supports physical, digital, service, license, edition, subscription, commission, and hybrid offers without making any one type the visual default.

### 22.3 Organizational Scale

Future records may distinguish:

- creator or author
- studio
- collaborator
- manufacturer
- fabricator
- publisher
- distributor
- service lead
- rights holder
- support owner

The current personal identity must not be hard-coded in a way that prevents shared authorship or organizational evolution.

### 22.4 Operational Scale

Future implementation may need to support:

- inventory
- production queues
- service capacity
- bookings
- subscriptions
- digital entitlements
- license seats
- versioning
- order management
- invoices
- fulfilment tracking
- customer records
- support references
- regional rules

These capabilities should attach to the approved product model rather than redefining the district.

### 22.5 Geographic Scale

The system should be capable of handling:

- multiple currencies
- translated information
- regional availability
- shipping zones
- local taxes
- measurement conventions
- region-specific licenses or policies

Regional differences must be explicit.

### 22.6 Studio Identity

As the platform grows, Products may become the commercial interface of a studio. Even then, commerce should remain one expression of the larger practice rather than its complete identity.

### 22.7 Archive as Institutional Memory

The archive should preserve:

- product identity
- origin relationship
- release context
- specifications
- edition or version history
- prior availability state
- successor relationships
- support relevance

Commercial retirement must not erase design history.

## 25. Approved Foundational Decisions

1. **Products operationalizes value.**
2. Products is the platform’s commercial district, not a detached shop and not the governing identity of the platform.
3. A product is a resolved and responsibly offerable form of value with defined deliverables, conditions, provision, and support.
4. Work, Research, Lab, and Art retain canonical ownership of their respective records; Products owns the commercial offer and transaction layer.
5. An artwork remains canonical in Art even when it becomes available as an original, edition, license, or commission.
6. An experiment becomes offerable only when a defined version crosses a threshold of value, stability, provision, commercial clarity, support, and lifecycle responsibility.
7. The district supports digital products, physical objects, services, licenses, editions, subscriptions, commissions, and hybrid offers.
8. Its spatial concept is **The Quiet Exchange Hall**, combining showroom, catalogue archive, evaluation table, and counter.
9. The homepage remains curated and atmospheric; the catalogue carries comprehensive discovery functions.
10. Product pages present essential commercial information before extended narrative context.
11. Pricing, availability, fulfilment, licensing, recurring conditions, and support are primary information layers.
12. The visitor journey proceeds from interest to orientation, evaluation, selection, commitment, review, confirmation, fulfilment, and support.
13. Commerce must not use false urgency, hidden costs, manipulative defaults, obstructive cancellation, or misleading scarcity.
14. Products uses explicit lifecycle states for forthcoming, available, limited, made-to-order, preorder, temporarily unavailable, capacity closed, sold out, discontinued, superseded, archived, regionally unavailable, and free-access offers.
15. Inactive products remain accessible when they have documentary, provenance, compatibility, or support value.
16. Architectural identity is expressed through hierarchy, rhythm, spatial silence, cataloguing, and measured transitions—not through a generic e-commerce template or literal shop simulation.
17. The architecture must scale from a small personal catalogue to a multi-author studio or company without foundational redesign.
18. Commercial retirement must not erase the practice’s creative, professional, experimental, or institutional history.
