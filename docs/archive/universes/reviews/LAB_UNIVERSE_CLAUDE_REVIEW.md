## 1. Definition

**Lab tests and transforms.**

The Lab is where ideas become observable tests: experiments, prototypes, simulations, comparisons, models, failed attempts, and abandoned directions whose value lies in what they reveal. It is not storage for unfinished work — it is a curated environment for disciplined uncertainty.

> Research asks and interprets. Lab tests and transforms. Work resolves and demonstrates.

Lab sits between Research's intellectual framing and Work's or Products' accountable outcomes — where a question becomes operational without being forced prematurely into resolution.

---

## 2. Governing Concept — The Open Test Structure

An unfinished but carefully ordered environment where experiments can be assembled, altered, compared, branched, suspended, and transferred. **Test structure**, because every public entry organizes around a deliberate experimental act. **Open structure**, because it preserves uncertainty, revision, and future extension.

Experimentation here is not disorder — it is an architecture capable of change. The experience is not browsing incomplete projects. It is entering a field where transformation has left visible traces.

---

## 3. Purpose

Lab gives experimentation a canonical home so it isn't forced into Work for looking impressive or into Research for originating in a question; makes learning-through-making visible; preserves meaningful incompleteness without presenting it as defective; keeps iteration readable as lineage rather than a disappearing history; and connects to every adjacent district without duplicating any of them.

---

## 4. Position Within the Platform

```text
Research asks and interprets.
        ↓
Lab tests and transforms.
        ↓
Work resolves and demonstrates.
```

Not strictly linear — a Lab result can just as easily send the visitor back to Research:

```text
Research question → Lab experiment → Observed result → Revised research question
```

Not every experiment must mature into Work or Products. Some exist to reveal a limitation, invalidate an assumption, or deliberately end a direction — and that is a complete, legitimate outcome.

---

## 5. What Qualifies as an Experiment

A public Lab entry must contain all four elements. This is the district's actual gate, and it is worth keeping exactly as approved:

1. **A framed uncertainty** — a question, condition, or possibility not already resolved.
2. **A deliberate transformation** — something built, altered, simulated, compared, or measured.
3. **Observable evidence** — behavior, output, comparison, failure, or trace that can be inspected.
4. **A meaningful interpretation or open edge** — what became visible, and what remains unresolved.

Small, early, rough, or unsuccessful work still qualifies when these four conditions hold. What does **not** qualify automatically: an undeveloped idea, a raw sketch with no defined test, a tutorial exercise, an isolated technical demo with no relevance to the practice, or a product already presented primarily as an offer.

**Ownership tests against the neighbors:**

- **vs. Research** — Research owns framing, interpretation, and argument. Lab owns setup, transformation, and result.
- **vs. Work** — ownership follows whether the dominant public meaning is the test-and-learning process or the resolved outcome. Technology never decides this; state and purpose do.
- **vs. Products** — Lab while the value is experimental behavior or proof of possibility; Products once it becomes a defined proposition for sustained external use.

---

## 6. Core Identity

**Experimental architectural intelligence** — architecture as a way of structuring transformation: conditions framed, elements assembled, relationships altered, behavior observed, versions compared.

The Lab should feel investigative, precise, provisional, and candid. It should not feel chaotic, novelty-driven, like a coding playground, or like Work with weaker presentation — that last one matters most: a common failure mode is dressing up an unfinished project as an experiment to avoid the higher bar Work sets for resolution.

---

## 7. Spatial Archetype

An **unfinished testing hall** — informed by a model room, a prototyping workshop after hours, a calibration chamber, an unfinished construction floor. Not a literal laboratory.

Unfinished here should feel open rather than broken, adaptable rather than temporary, inhabited by process rather than abandoned mid-construction. Exposed structures reveal how the environment holds together. Empty test bays communicate future capacity, not missing content — the environment must never resemble a damaged page or an implementation placeholder.

---

## 8. Atmosphere

Calm, nocturnal, analytical, active, and slightly liminal — work is visibly occurring, but the visitor hasn't entered a control room or a spectacle of technology.

Relative to its neighbors: Research is slower and more diffuse; Work is more stable and resolved; **Lab is the most visibly changeable of the three without becoming the loudest.**

Light behaves as a tool of examination — active tests get localized clarity, archived material recedes without disappearing. Silence remains the baseline inherited from Home; energy comes from shifting focus and visible comparison, never from constant motion or density.

---

## 9. Energetic Without Chaotic

Only a limited number of experiments occupy the foreground at once; the wider archive stays accessible but quieter. Information discloses in layers — orientation, signal, question, structure, evidence, archive. Every visible connection or branch must represent a real experimental relationship; decorative complexity is prohibited. Growth is managed through zoning and recession, never by compressing everything into an identical card grid.

---

## 10. Experience Narrative

Arrival → Orientation → Approach → Examination → Tracing → Return.

The threshold states: **Lab — where questions become tests and transformations.** Approaching an experiment brings its question and state into focus; entering it reveals versions, branches, and open edges; the visitor can move backward to the Research question, sideways to sibling experiments, or outward to a mature Work or Product entry. Return restores the visitor's previous position and lens — the Lab supports memory of process.

---

## 11. Homepage

**Sequence:** Threshold → Active Assembly → Experimental Field → Lineage Junctions → Archive Depth → Transfer Edge.

- **Threshold** confirms location; provides `Explore`, `Experiments`, `Lineages`, `Index`, `Search`.
- **Active Assembly** — a deliberately small set of active or recently meaningful experiments. Not a news feed; selection is based on experimental significance, not recency.
- **Experimental Field** — the broader organized field: related questions, shared methods, families, quiet archival depth.
- **Lineage Junctions** — origins, branches, convergences, and transfers made visible as spatial structure, not a chronological list.
- **Archive Depth** — the complete indexed collection: scanning, filtering, chronology, status, search.
- **Transfer Edge** — real passages toward Research, Work, Products, and Art. Not a recommendation carousel — actual maturation and lineage.

---

## 12. Navigation Model

Overview · Experiments · Field (atmospheric, spatial) · Constellations (curated readings by shared question, method, or tension — not permanent departments) · Lineages (the Lab's principal model of iteration) · Timeline · Index · Search.

A search result should explain what matched, what is being tested, its current state, and whether it originates in Research or has transferred elsewhere. Visitors must always understand where they are, why items are grouped, and how the current experiment connects to the wider platform.

---

## 13. Iteration as Spatial Structure

Iteration is the Lab's defining behavior — never reduced to a version dropdown or a sequence of screenshots.

**The experimental spine:**

```text
Premise → Test 01 → Observation → Revision → Test 02 → Current outcome
```

Includes only changes that alter meaning, behavior, or direction — not every saved file.

- **Branches** — a meaningful alternative tested without replacing the original path; stays connected to its origin.
- **Versions** — a significant state, shown when comparison clarifies what changed and why. Routine saves and cosmetic tweaks are not public versions.
- **Tests** — a bounded act with a stated condition: what was varied, what was held fixed, what was observed.
- **Convergence** — branches merging into a shared method without erasing the branches that produced it.
- **Dead ends** — a direction terminates when the hypothesis fails or the evidence runs out; it stays visible when its ending contributes useful knowledge.

---

## 14. Experiment States

The approved lifecycle — describing **condition, not quality or prestige**:

**Framed** (question defined, no evidence yet) → **Trial** (first observable evidence) → **Active** (ongoing development) → **Branching** (parallel directions) → **Stabilized** (repeatable behavior; not the same as market-ready) → **Paused** (intentionally suspended, reason legible) → **Concluded** (question answered for current scope) → **Abandoned** (deliberately discontinued, reason stated openly) → **Transferred** (mature continuation exists in Work, Products, Art, or Research — the Lab record stays canonical for the process) → **Archived** (no longer active, remains part of public memory).

---

## 15. Presenting Unstable, Failed, and Abandoned Work

The district's most distinctive commitment: uncertainty is represented honestly without becoming careless.

- **Unstable** — identify the instability, the conditions under which it appears, and the current confidence level. Never dramatized as mystery.
- **Incomplete** — separate what has been tested from what hasn't. Incomplete does not mean undocumented.
- **Failed** — show intended behavior, observed behavior, the point of divergence, and the resulting decision. Not romanticized — failure is valuable only when it produces understanding.
- **Invalid** — state plainly why no reliable conclusion can be drawn; never presented as failure or success.
- **Abandoned** — stays visible when its termination explains a decision or prevents repetition.

**Curatorial threshold:** this material becomes public only once documented enough to communicate what was attempted, what occurred, and why it matters. Raw fragments stay latent. Every state receives the same editorial care — a failed experiment should never look broken, dimmed into illegibility, or played for comic relief.

---

## 16. Organization of Experiments

One canonical record per experiment, organized across overlapping dimensions rather than one rigid taxonomy: **Question** (the most important dimension — what uncertainty is being tested), **Territory**, **Method**, **Medium**, **State**, **Outcome**, **Lineage**, **Connected universe**.

Two layers exist: **public** experiments (curated, documented, intelligible) and **latent** experiments (private, raw, not yet suitable for presentation). The public Lab is not the complete working archive.

---

## 17. Anatomy of an Experiment Record

An information obligation, not a page template: Identity → Origin (source question, connected Research line) → Experimental question → Setup (inputs, constraints, variables) → Transformation → Evidence → Observation → Outcome → Iteration structure → Open edge → Transfers.

**Content hierarchy** for what matters most, top to bottom: Question, Test condition, Transformation, Evidence, Observation, Outcome, Iteration, Continuation. Visual output may attract attention. It must never replace the question, setup, or learning.

---

## 18. Visual Language

The Lab develops the platform's shared architectural vocabulary through measurement, registration, comparison, and trace — calibration grids, test bays, datum lines, branching structures, exploded relations. Geometry must communicate structure or state; decorative pseudo-technical graphics are prohibited.

**Line roles:** structural (stable frame), test (experimental boundary), trace (development record), branch (divergence), comparison (aligned alternatives), transfer (connection to another district), open (unresolved continuation), terminated (concluded or abandoned, without erasure).

Any Lab-specific color accent is used sparingly and never carries state alone. Typography stays governed by the shared Design System; an operational annotation layer (identifiers, version labels, states) remains subordinate to normal reading — the Lab should not imitate a terminal or dashboard without conceptual necessity. *(Exact tokens, colors, and values remain governed by the Design System throughout this section — stated once here rather than after every subsection.)*

---

## 19. Interaction Philosophy

Interaction feels like examining, calibrating, and tracing. Focusing an experiment brings its question and evidence into clarity while unrelated material recedes; information discloses progressively (Signal → Question → Setup → Evidence → Observation → Outcome → Open edge); comparison between versions, branches, and expected-vs-observed behavior is a core experiential behavior, not a secondary feature.

Motion favors line extension, layer separation, and measured translation — never constant ambient animation, decorative particles, or motion implying activity where none exists. Interactions stay reversible; reduced-motion and direct-navigation paths retain the same hierarchy.

---

## 20. Movement to Research, Work, and Products

| Passage | Communicates |
|---|---|
| Research → Lab | *This question is being tested here.* |
| Lab → Research | *This result changed the inquiry.* |
| Lab → Work | *This experiment became consequential in practice.* |
| Lab → Products | *This prototype became a usable proposition.* |

**Maturation into Work** requires more than technical success — a meaningful applied context. The Lab record stays canonical for origin, versions, and failures; the Work entry becomes canonical for context, role, and resolved outcome. Approved relationship labels: `Developed into`, `Applied in`, `Tested through`, `Earlier iteration`, `Resulted in`, `Shared method`.

**Maturation into Products** requires a defined user, purpose, boundary, and support expectation — a working prototype alone is not enough. The Lab retains prototype history and tested alternatives; Products owns the proposition and access conditions.

A failed or abandoned experiment can still inform Work or Products by revealing what not to use — stated precisely, never presented as a mature outcome.

**Art:** a Lab process may produce an autonomous artwork. Lab owns the experiment; Art owns the resulting work as an experience in its own right — the relationship should never force Art to justify itself as technical evidence.

---

## 21. Tone of Voice

Direct, precise, candid, observational: *tested, varied, held constant, generated, compared, failed, stabilized, branched, remains unresolved.* Avoid presenting every experiment as innovation, startup hype, claims of completion where uncertainty remains, or romanticizing unfinished work. The Lab speaks as a reflective practitioner documenting transformation — not a demo reel.

---

## 22. Responsive Behavior and Accessibility

Responsive behavior preserves relationships and sequence rather than shrinking the desktop field. On small screens, the Lab becomes a **vertical assembly sequence** — ordered sections, explicit relationship labels, expandable lineages — never an undifferentiated list of identical cards.

The Lab must remain fully usable without atmospheric motion, hover, or spatial visualization: every relationship has a semantic equivalent, every state is expressed in text (never color alone), comparisons read in linear order, and the Index provides a complete direct-access mode. This is part of the Lab concept, not a fallback representation.

*(Merges v1.0 §26 and §27, which separately described the same graceful-degradation behavior for screen size and for assistive technology.)*

---

## 23. Scalability

Coherent with ten experiments or several hundred. Early on, the Lab foregrounds a limited number and treats empty bays as genuine future capacity — never simulating an archive larger than what exists. As the collection grows, the separation between Active Assembly, Experimental Field, Lineages, and the complete Index becomes increasingly load-bearing.

**Stable regardless of scale:** experiment as the canonical entity, question before novelty, the four-part qualification test, lineages as the model of iteration, the public/latent distinction, and clear cross-universe ownership. Growth adds experiments and relationships. It does not increase visual noise.

---

## 24. Anti-Patterns

A dumping ground for unfinished work. A chronological process blog. A coding-exercise archive. A social-media feed. A decorative node network. A duplicate of Research. A weaker version of Work. A celebration of failure without interpretation. An environment where complexity substitutes for rigor.

## 27. Final Definition
