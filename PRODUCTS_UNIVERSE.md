# Personal Platform — Products Universe

**Document:** `PRODUCTS_UNIVERSE.md`  
**Status:** Proposal  
**Domain owner:** Products Universe conversation  
**Scope:** Storefront philosophy, identity, layouts, user experience, navigation, product hierarchy, and future scalability  
**Exclusions:** Product creation, product naming, pricing, inventory decisions, payment-provider selection, and technical implementation

---

## 1. Purpose

The Products Universe is the commercial district of the Personal Platform.

It is where designed outcomes can eventually be discovered, understood, acquired, downloaded, licensed, reserved, or ordered. It must support commerce without reducing the wider platform to an online shop.

The district should communicate that every product is part of the same design practice represented across Work, Research, Lab, and Art. Products are not detached merchandise. They are resolved objects, tools, publications, editions, or systems that have crossed from exploration into a form that can be offered to others.

The Products Universe must therefore operate simultaneously as:

- a curated showroom
- a navigable catalogue
- a trustworthy storefront
- a record of released work
- a scalable foundation for future commercial activity

It should remain useful when the catalogue is small, and remain coherent if it grows substantially.

---

## 2. Position Within the Platform

The Products Universe is one district inside the shared architectural metaverse.

It should not feel like an external shop attached to the website. Entering Products should feel like moving from the platform’s common world into a more precise, ordered, and transactional environment.

Its relationship to the other districts is distinct:

- **Work** demonstrates professional capability and completed commissions.
- **Research** develops questions, arguments, and knowledge.
- **Lab** exposes experiments, prototypes, and unfinished investigations.
- **Art** supports contemplation, interpretation, and visual experience.
- **Products** presents outcomes that are sufficiently resolved to be offered.

A single body of work may appear across several districts, but each district must present it according to its own purpose. Products should not duplicate an entire project narrative. It should provide the commercial endpoint and link back to the relevant origin, process, research, or artistic context when useful.

## 3. Core Concept

### The Quiet Exchange Hall

The Products Universe is conceived as a quiet exchange hall within the larger architectural world.

It combines three spatial ideas:

1. **The showroom** — selected items are given space, scale, and presence.
2. **The archive** — the complete catalogue is ordered, searchable, and durable.
3. **The counter** — purchasing information and actions are direct, legible, and trustworthy.

The experience should feel curated rather than crowded, exact rather than promotional, and calm rather than passive.

The architecture of the district should suggest:

- measured display bays
- modular shelving systems
- specimen tables
- inventory markings
- elevation and section logic
- serial organization
- controlled thresholds
- carefully framed objects

These references should guide composition and behavior without turning the interface into a literal shop interior.

## 4. Storefront Philosophy

### 4.1 Curation Before Volume

The storefront should feel intentionally edited even when the catalogue grows.

The homepage must not attempt to show everything. It should establish the current selection, orient the visitor, and provide a clear route into the full catalogue.

Curation may later be organized through collections, themes, releases, formats, or temporary groupings. The system must allow these layers without requiring the permanent product taxonomy to change.

### 4.2 Understanding Before Conversion

The visitor should be able to understand an offering before being pushed toward a transaction.

Every product presentation should answer, in a logical sequence:

- What is it?
- Why does it exist?
- What does it include?
- How is it used, received, or delivered?
- What are its important specifications or conditions?
- What does it cost?
- What action can the visitor take?

The interface should not obscure essential information behind promotional language or interaction effects.

### 4.3 Commerce Without Pressure

The Products Universe should reject artificial urgency and manipulative retail patterns.

It should not rely on:

- countdown timers without a real operational reason
- false scarcity
- automatically selected extras
- hidden costs
- confusing cancellation paths
- excessive pop-ups
- aggressive cross-selling
- visually dominant discount language

Real constraints—such as limited editions, release periods, production lead times, or stock levels—should be stated plainly and accurately.

### 4.4 Context Adds Value

Products should be presented as designed outcomes with origins, decisions, and intended use—not as isolated thumbnails.

Context may include links to:

- the project from which the product emerged
- the research question it addresses
- the experiment that produced it
- the artistic series to which it belongs
- the design or fabrication principles behind it

This context should deepen understanding without obstructing a visitor who already intends to buy.

### 4.5 Trust Is Part of the Design

Commercial trust should be visible throughout the journey rather than concentrated at checkout.

The storefront should make the following easy to locate and understand when relevant:

- price and currency
- taxes or tax treatment
- delivery or access method
- expected timing
- licensing terms
- edition or availability status
- returns, cancellation, or refund conditions
- privacy and payment security information
- seller or studio identity
- contact and support routes

The visual system should treat this information as part of the product experience, not as secondary legal clutter.

### 4.6 The Storefront Is Not the Entire Identity

Commercial performance matters, but it should not dominate the character of the Personal Platform.

The Products Universe should support transactions while preserving the broader position of the platform as a coherent professional and creative practice.

## 5. Identity and Atmosphere

The Products Universe should feel:

- precise
- composed
- tactile
- ordered
- calm
- credible
- quietly desirable

Its identity should sit between the contemplative atmosphere of Art and the systematic clarity of Work.

Compared with other districts:

- it is more resolved and less experimental than Lab
- more transactional and less interpretive than Art
- more object-focused and less case-study-driven than Work
- more immediate and less discursive than Research

The shared platform design system should govern typography, color, spacing, accessibility, and motion. The Products Universe should create distinction through composition, rhythm, image treatment, information density, and interaction behavior rather than through an unrelated visual identity.

### Atmospheric Principles

- Products receive enough empty space to establish presence.
- Information is aligned and measured rather than scattered.
- Images reveal scale, material, interface, or use when relevant.
- Metadata can use catalogue, edition, or inventory logic.
- Motion should resemble revealing, sliding, unfolding, or moving between display bays.
- Transactional actions should remain stable and immediately legible.
- Decorative effects must never reduce confidence or clarity.

## 6. Experience Principles

### 6.1 Two Valid Visitor Modes

The district should support both:

- **Exploratory browsing:** visitors who want to discover what exists.
- **Directed acquisition:** visitors who already know what they are seeking.

The curated homepage serves the first mode. Search, filters, category navigation, and persistent purchase information serve the second.

Neither path should be treated as secondary.

### 6.2 Progressive Disclosure

The interface should reveal information in layers:

1. identity and essential proposition
2. key format, price, and availability
3. deeper description and specifications
4. provenance, process, or related context
5. policies and transaction details

Essential purchase information must never depend on opening optional narrative layers.

### 6.3 Stable Orientation

Visitors should always understand:

- that they are inside Products
- which collection or category they are viewing
- whether they are browsing a curated selection or the complete catalogue
- which filters are active
- how to return to the previous level
- the state of their cart or transaction

Spatial immersion must not weaken basic wayfinding.

### 6.4 One Primary Action Per State

Each major interface state should have one visually dominant action.

Examples of action types include:

- enter catalogue
- view a collection
- open product details
- add or reserve
- proceed to checkout
- access a purchased digital item

Secondary actions should remain available without competing with the primary next step.

### 6.5 Accessibility Is Structural

The storefront must remain operable without relying on hover, animation, fine pointer movement, color alone, or spatial interpretation.

The future system should support:

- clear keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- semantic page hierarchy
- descriptive product imagery
- legible pricing and status text
- reduced-motion behavior
- zoom and text resizing
- sufficiently large interactive targets
- comprehensible validation and error messages
- accessible cart and checkout sequences

Immersion is optional. Access is not.

## 7. Products Universe Homepage

The homepage should function as the threshold to the commercial district, not as a compressed catalogue.

Its purpose is to:

- establish the character of the storefront
- communicate what kinds of outcomes belong here
- present a controlled current selection
- expose the main catalogue structure
- direct visitors toward either exploration or acquisition

### Proposed Homepage Sequence

#### 7.1 Threshold

A restrained opening field introduces Products as a district.

It should contain:

- district name
- concise positioning statement
- one primary route into the catalogue
- one secondary route into the current curated selection

The threshold may use architectural framing, scale, or linework, but it should not resemble a conventional promotional banner.

#### 7.2 Current Display

A limited set of future featured entries occupies a spacious display zone.

This area is not permanently tied to “new arrivals.” It may later hold:

- a current release
- a curated collection
- a highlighted edition
- a seasonal grouping
- a selected digital tool
- an editorial product story

The module should remain valid even when only one item is featured.

#### 7.3 Catalogue Plane

The permanent organizational structure becomes visible.

This zone should provide direct access to:

- all products
- primary product families
- collections
- availability or release states when useful

It should feel like a navigational drawing or index rather than a decorative category grid.

#### 7.4 Collection Corridors

Selected collections may be introduced as distinct spatial sequences.

Each collection entry should communicate:

- collection identity
- organizing idea
- approximate scope
- route to the collection page

The homepage should show only a controlled number of collection corridors. The complete collection index belongs deeper in the district.

#### 7.5 Practice Connections

A contextual zone links products back to the wider platform.

This section may explain that products emerge from the same practice represented across Work, Research, Lab, and Art. It should use a few meaningful cross-links rather than a generic feed from every district.

#### 7.6 Store Information

The closing zone should provide calm access to practical information such as:

- delivery and access
- licensing
- returns or refunds
- support
- seller information
- frequently needed commercial policies

This information should be reachable without forcing the visitor to begin checkout.

## 8. Page and Layout System

The Products Universe should be built from a limited family of page types. New products and collections should populate these structures rather than generate a new layout each time.

### 8.1 Products Homepage

**Purpose:** Curated entry and orientation.

**Layout character:** Spacious, editorial, selective, and atmospheric.

**Primary content:** Threshold, current display, catalogue entry points, selected collections, practice connections, store information.

### 8.2 Catalogue Index

**Purpose:** Show the complete current catalogue.

**Layout character:** Denser, systematic, and highly navigable.

**Required capabilities:**

- clear total scope
- sorting
- filtering
- search when catalogue size justifies it
- active-filter visibility
- empty-state recovery
- consistent product summaries

The catalogue should support both visual and compact browsing modes in the future, but a single clear default should be established before adding alternatives.

### 8.3 Product Family or Category Page

**Purpose:** Group products through a durable classification.

**Layout character:** A clear index preceded by a concise definition of the family.

A category page should not require a custom campaign identity. Its role is structural.

### 8.4 Collection Page

**Purpose:** Present a curated grouping united by a theme, release, context, or editorial idea.

**Layout character:** More narrative than a category page, but more concise than a project case study.

A collection can be temporary or permanent. Removing it from featured navigation must not remove its products from the catalogue.

### 8.5 Product Detail Page

**Purpose:** Support understanding, evaluation, and action.

**Proposed information sequence:**

1. product identity
2. essential visual or interactive representation
3. concise proposition
4. price and availability
5. primary action
6. included contents or variants
7. specifications
8. delivery, access, or licensing information
9. extended description
10. origin, process, or related practice context
11. support and policies
12. related entries, used sparingly

On wider screens, the primary action and essential purchase information may remain stable while deeper content is explored. On smaller screens, the same hierarchy should become a clear linear sequence without hiding essential information in accordions by default.

### 8.6 Cart or Selection Space

**Purpose:** Review intended acquisitions before checkout.

**Layout character:** Quiet, compact, editable, and transparent.

It should clearly show:

- selected entries
- relevant format or variant
- quantity where applicable
- individual and combined cost
- estimated additional charges when known
- delivery or access distinctions
- removal and editing controls
- next action

The cart should not become a promotional page.

### 8.7 Checkout

**Purpose:** Complete the transaction with minimum uncertainty.

**Layout character:** Focused and linear.

Global exploration should recede during checkout, while essential identity, help, policy, and safe-exit routes remain available.

The exact checkout structure depends on future technical, legal, geographic, and payment decisions and is therefore not defined in this document.

### 8.8 Release Archive

**Purpose:** Preserve the history of products, editions, and releases that are no longer active.

**Layout character:** Documentary rather than promotional.

Archived entries should remain understandable and linkable. Their status must be explicit so they cannot be mistaken for currently available products.

### 8.9 Store Information Pages

**Purpose:** Centralize stable commercial guidance.

Potential page families include delivery, digital access, licensing, returns, support, privacy, and terms. Their exact legal content is outside this document.

## 9. Product Presentation System

### 9.1 Product Summary Unit

The reusable product summary should contain only the information needed to decide whether to open the detail page.

Its future fields may include:

- primary image or preview
- product name
- product family or format
- concise descriptor
- price or price state
- availability state
- edition or release marker when relevant

The unit should not become a miniature product page.

### 9.2 Image and Media Roles

Product media should be selected by informational purpose rather than quantity.

Possible roles include:

- identity image
- scale image
- detail image
- use-context image
- interface preview
- contents overview
- material or fabrication detail
- motion or interaction demonstration

The system should support different media requirements for physical, digital, and publication-based products while preserving a consistent page hierarchy.

### 9.3 Specifications as Architecture

Specifications should be treated as an organized information layer, not as a block of small print.

Depending on the future product type, specifications may include dimensions, file formats, compatibility, materials, edition details, language, access period, license, version, or delivery method.

Only applicable fields should be shown. Empty or irrelevant specification rows should never appear.

### 9.4 Status Language

Availability must use a controlled vocabulary.

The system should be capable of supporting states such as:

- available
- temporarily unavailable
- upcoming
- preorder
- made to order
- limited availability
- sold out
- retired
- archived
- free access
- unavailable in a region

The final vocabulary should be approved before implementation. Status labels must represent real operational conditions.

## 10. Navigation Architecture

Navigation should operate at three interconnected levels.

### 10.1 Global Platform Navigation

Products remains one district in the shared global navigation.

The global system must provide:

- entry to Products from anywhere in the platform
- a clear route back to Home
- access to the other major districts
- recognition that the visitor remains inside the same personal universe

The Products Universe should not replace the global navigation with an unrelated e-commerce header.

### 10.2 Local Products Navigation

The local navigation should support the commercial structure without becoming overloaded.

Proposed primary destinations:

- Products overview
- All products
- Product families
- Collections
- Archive
- Store information

Search and cart should become persistent utilities when they are operationally justified.

Local navigation may change density between the atmospheric homepage and the systematic catalogue, but labels and hierarchy must remain stable.

### 10.3 Contextual Navigation

Contextual links should connect:

- a product to its collection
- a product to its category
- a product to related Work, Research, Lab, or Art context
- a collection back to the catalogue
- an archived item to its relevant active successor, when one exists

Cross-universe links should explain the relationship. A visitor should not be moved unexpectedly into another district.

## 11. Product Hierarchy

The hierarchy must separate permanent structure from temporary curation.

### Level 0 — Products Universe

The commercial district as a whole.

### Level 1 — Catalogue and Curated Entry Points

Two parallel systems:

- **Catalogue:** the complete set of product records.
- **Curated entry points:** selected collections, releases, or editorial displays.

The catalogue is permanent. Curated groupings can change.

### Level 2 — Product Families

Durable classifications based on what an offering fundamentally is or how it is delivered.

The exact families are not approved here. The system should be capable of accommodating future formats such as physical objects, digital products, publications, editions, licenses, or other resolved offerings without making any one of them the default identity of Products.

A product should have one primary family whenever possible. Multiple overlapping tags may support discovery without replacing the main classification.

### Level 3 — Collections

Curated groupings based on concept, release, origin, audience, theme, or moment.

A product may belong to multiple collections. Collections must not determine inventory ownership or canonical URLs.

### Level 4 — Product Record

The canonical detail page and source of product truth.

Each product record should contain the structured information required to present, filter, transact, archive, and cross-reference the offering.

### Level 5 — Variants or Editions

Specific purchasable forms of a product, where needed.

Variants should be used only when the options belong to the same core product. Fundamentally different offerings should receive separate product records.

### Supporting Classification — Attributes and Tags

Attributes enable filtering and comparison. Tags support looser discovery and editorial connections.

They should not be exposed as an uncontrolled cloud of labels.

## 12. Browsing, Search, and Filtering

### 12.1 Browse First, Search When Needed

A small catalogue should not imitate the complexity of a large marketplace.

Search and advanced filtering should appear when the number and diversity of entries make them genuinely useful. The underlying information model should support them from the beginning, even if the interface introduces them later.

### 12.2 Filter Dimensions

Potential future filter dimensions include:

- product family
- format
- availability
- collection
- discipline or origin district
- delivery method
- edition type
- price range

Only dimensions with enough meaningful variation should be exposed.

### 12.3 Filter Behavior

Filters should:

- show their effect immediately or through a clear apply action
- remain visible after results update
- be individually removable
- provide a complete reset
- preserve the visitor’s place when returning from a product page
- produce an understandable empty state
- be encoded in a shareable state when technically feasible

### 12.4 Search Behavior

Search should prioritize exact and meaningful matches across product names, descriptions, categories, collections, and structured attributes.

The system should distinguish between no results and unavailable results. Archived products may appear when relevant, but must be clearly marked.

## 13. Transactional User Journey

The Products Universe should support a stable sequence:

1. **Arrival** — enter through the district homepage, a direct product link, a collection, or a cross-universe reference.
2. **Orientation** — understand location, product type, availability, and next routes.
3. **Evaluation** — review proposition, media, specifications, context, terms, and price.
4. **Selection** — choose the appropriate format, edition, license, or variant when applicable.
5. **Review** — verify the selection and total conditions.
6. **Checkout** — provide only the information necessary to complete the transaction.
7. **Confirmation** — receive an explicit record of what happens next.
8. **Fulfilment or access** — obtain delivery, download, license, reservation, or order information.
9. **Support** — find help without reconstructing the original journey.

Each stage should clearly state the current condition and next action. Error recovery should preserve entered information whenever safe and appropriate.

## 14. Empty, Early, and Transitional States

The Products Universe must remain intentional before a large catalogue exists.

### 14.1 Before Launch

A controlled district threshold may explain that the storefront is being prepared, with no false product placeholders or artificial inventory.

### 14.2 Small Catalogue

When only a few products exist, each can receive generous presentation. The interface should not display empty category grids, unnecessary filters, or inflated collection structures.

### 14.3 No Matching Results

The visitor should see:

- which conditions produced no results
- an immediate way to clear or change them
- a route back to the complete catalogue

### 14.4 Unavailable Product

The product record may remain visible if it has documentary value. The page should clearly distinguish temporary unavailability, sold-out status, retirement, and archive status.

### 14.5 Cart and Checkout Failure

Failures should use direct language, identify the affected step, preserve valid selections, and provide a clear recovery or support route.

## 15. Responsive Behavior

The Products Universe should preserve its hierarchy across devices rather than reproduce the desktop composition at reduced scale.

### Wide Screens

- Use space to establish product presence.
- Allow catalogue and detail information to occupy coordinated columns.
- Keep essential purchase information stable while supporting deeper reading.
- Use horizontal movement only when it has an equivalent, obvious control and does not conceal catalogue scope.

### Medium Screens

- Reduce simultaneous information zones.
- Preserve the relationship between media, essential details, and primary action.
- Convert complex index structures into clearer stacked groups.

### Small Screens

- Use a deliberate linear sequence.
- Keep price, availability, and primary action easy to find.
- Avoid hover-dependent disclosure.
- Ensure media does not displace essential information excessively.
- Make filters, variants, and cart editing usable with touch and assistive technology.

The mobile experience should remain a designed storefront, not a collapsed desktop page.

---

## 16. Motion and Interaction

Motion should reinforce spatial organization and object inspection.

Appropriate behaviors may include:

- controlled reveals between display zones
- subtle transitions from summary to detail
- measured image sequencing
- unfolding specification layers
- clear state changes when an item is selected
- restrained feedback when cart or availability status changes

Motion should not delay access to product information or transactional controls.

The district should always provide a reduced-motion experience with equivalent information and functionality.

## 17. Relationship to Content and Editorial Strategy

Products should be able to participate in editorial storytelling without turning the storefront into a magazine.

The system may later support:

- release notes
- collection introductions
- product origin stories
- process excerpts
- care or use guides
- version histories
- related research or project links

Editorial content should remain modular. A product record must still function when no extended story is present.

## 18. Future Scalability

### 18.1 Catalogue Scale

The architecture should support growth from a few entries to a substantial catalogue through:

- canonical product records
- stable product families
- optional collections
- controlled attributes
- lifecycle states
- structured media roles
- reusable page types

Growth should add content and navigation depth, not require a visual redesign.

### 18.2 Product-Type Scale

The data and layout system should accommodate materially different offering types, including future physical, digital, publication, edition, or license-based products.

Shared core information should remain consistent, while type-specific modules appear only where relevant.

### 18.3 Commercial Model Scale

The framework should not assume that every future offering uses the same transaction model.

It should be capable of expanding to support, if approved later:

- immediate purchase
- free acquisition
- reservation
- preorder
- made-to-order requests
- limited editions
- digital delivery
- license selection
- recurring access
- business or institutional purchasing

These are future capabilities, not approved launch requirements.

### 18.4 Geographic Scale

Future expansion may require:

- multiple currencies
- regional availability
- localized taxes
- shipping zones
- translated product information
- region-specific policies
- local measurement conventions

The interface should expose regional differences clearly rather than silently changing essential conditions.

### 18.5 Operational Scale

As commercial activity grows, the storefront may need to support:

- inventory states
- order management
- fulfilment tracking
- customer support references
- product versions
- edition numbering
- downloadable files
- access permissions
- invoice information

These capabilities belong to future platform architecture and implementation planning. They should not determine the initial visual concept.

### 18.6 Organizational Scale

The Products Universe should remain valid if the personal platform later becomes a studio or company platform.

Future records may need to distinguish authorship, collaborators, manufacturers, publishers, distributors, or rights holders. The current identity should not be hard-coded in a way that prevents this evolution.

### 18.7 Archive Scale

Products should have a lifecycle rather than disappearing when no longer sold.

The archive should preserve:

- canonical product identity
- release context
- important specifications
- edition or version history
- original availability status
- links to related active work

Commercial retirement should not erase the platform’s design history.

## 19. System Boundaries

The Products Universe owns:

- storefront concept and atmosphere
- Products homepage structure
- local navigation model
- catalogue and collection logic
- product presentation hierarchy
- conceptual transaction journey
- product lifecycle and archive principles
- scalability requirements specific to commerce

It does not independently own:

- the global sitemap
- the shared design system
- global navigation behavior
- legal policy content
- payment infrastructure
- account architecture
- analytics strategy
- product ideation
- pricing strategy
- inventory operations
- fulfilment operations
- technical implementation

Changes to those areas must be coordinated with their relevant source-of-truth conversations.

## 20. Cross-Domain Dependencies

### Platform Vision

Must confirm the role of commercial activity within the wider personal brand and long-term future-company ambition.

### Information Architecture

Must define canonical URLs, global navigation, search scope, cross-universe relationships, and whether cart or account utilities are platform-wide or Products-specific.

### Design System

Must establish typography, color, spacing, grids, buttons, form controls, cards, motion, accessibility, and responsive rules used by the storefront.

### Home Universe

Must define how the visitor transitions from the shared entrance into the Products district and how the district is represented spatially within the metaverse.

### Work, Research, Lab, and Art Universes

Must agree on cross-linking rules so that origin stories and related context are connected without duplicating canonical content.

### About and Legal Structure

Must provide the seller identity, support routes, commercial policies, privacy information, and legally required disclosures.

### Technical Architecture and Claude Code

Must later translate approved structures into small, sequential, testable implementation milestones without redefining the experience.

## 21. Proposed Foundational Decisions

All items below remain **Proposal** until explicitly approved.

1. The Products Universe is the platform’s commercial district, not a detached online shop.
2. Its defining spatial concept is a quiet exchange hall combining showroom, archive, and counter.
3. The storefront prioritizes curation, understanding, transparency, and trust over promotional pressure.
4. Permanent catalogue structure is separated from temporary collections and featured displays.
5. Every product has one canonical record that supports presentation, transaction, lifecycle, archive, and cross-universe references.
6. The homepage remains selective and atmospheric; the catalogue index carries comprehensive browsing functions.
7. Product detail pages reveal essential commercial information before extended narrative context.
8. The system supports both exploratory browsing and directed acquisition.
9. Commerce uses no false urgency, hidden costs, manipulative defaults, or obstructive cancellation patterns.
10. The Products Universe preserves inactive and retired work through a clearly identified archive.
11. The framework must scale across catalogue size, product type, commercial model, geography, operations, and future organizational growth.
12. Shared design and global navigation remain governed by their respective project documents.

---

## 22. Recommended Approval Sequence

Before implementation planning begins, the Products Universe should be reviewed in this order:

1. Approve or revise the district’s role within the platform.
2. Approve or revise the Quiet Exchange Hall concept.
3. Approve the storefront philosophy and ethical commerce principles.
4. Approve the permanent product hierarchy.
5. Approve the homepage sequence and page-type family.
6. Coordinate navigation dependencies with Information Architecture.
7. Coordinate visual and interaction dependencies with the Design System.
8. Define the minimum launch state without creating individual products.
9. Convert the approved framework into small Claude Code milestones only after the preceding project phases permit implementation.

---

## 23. Completion Criteria for the Framework Phase

The Products Universe framework can be considered **Approved** when:

- its purpose and relationship to other districts are accepted
- its atmosphere and storefront philosophy are accepted
- the homepage structure is accepted
- the catalogue, category, collection, product, cart, archive, and information page roles are accepted
- the navigation hierarchy is aligned with Information Architecture
- the presentation system is aligned with the Design System
- product lifecycle states are defined
- launch scope and future capabilities are clearly separated
- no individual product content has been introduced

Implementation and verification are separate later statuses.
